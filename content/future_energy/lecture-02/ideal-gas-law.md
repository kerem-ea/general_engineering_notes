# Ideal Gas Law

## Equation of State

$$
pV = nRT
$$

Where:
- $p$ = absolute pressure ($\text{Pa} = \text{N/m}^2$)
- $V$ = volume ($\text{m}^3$)
- $n$ = amount of substance ($\text{mol}$)
- $R \approx 8.314\ \text{J/(mol}\cdot\text{K)}$ = universal gas constant
- $T$ = absolute temperature ($\text{K} = ^\circ\text{C} + 273.15$)

Specific volume form ($v = V/m$ and $R_{\mathrm{specific}} = R/M$):

$$
p\,v = R_{\mathrm{specific}}\,T
$$

## Microscopic Assumptions

1. **Point particles:** Gas molecules have negligible volume compared to container volume.
2. **No intermolecular forces:** No attractive or repulsive forces between particles except during collisions.
3. **Elastic collisions:** Collisions between particles and with walls conserve total kinetic energy.

## Isothermal Expansion Work

For an ideal gas at constant temperature ($T = \text{constant}$):

$$
W = \int_{V_1}^{V_2} p\,dV = nRT \int_{V_1}^{V_2} \frac{dV}{V} = nRT \ln\left(\frac{V_2}{V_1}\right)
$$
