# First Law of Thermodynamics and Entropy

## First law of thermodynamics

The first law expresses energy conservation for a thermodynamic system:

$$
\Delta U = Q - W_{\mathrm{by}}
$$

or equivalently, when defining work done *on* the system ($W_{\mathrm{on}} = -W_{\mathrm{by}}$):

$$
\Delta U = Q + W_{\mathrm{on}}
$$

where:

- $\Delta U$ = change in internal energy (a state function)
- $Q$ = heat transferred to the system (a path function)
- $W$ = mechanical work (a path function)

## Entropy and the second law

Entropy ($S$) measures the degree of disorder or the number of microscopic configurations accessible to a system:

$$
dS = \frac{\delta Q_{\mathrm{rev}}}{T}
$$

The **second law of thermodynamics** states that total entropy of an isolated system (the universe) never decreases over time:

$$
\Delta S_{\mathrm{universe}} \geq 0
$$

- For an ideal reversible process: $\Delta S_{\mathrm{universe}} = 0$
- For any natural, spontaneous irreversible process: $\Delta S_{\mathrm{universe}} > 0$

The second law explains the irreversible arrow of time and imposes the fundamental limit preventing 100% heat-to-work conversion.
