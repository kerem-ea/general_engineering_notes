# Energy, Work, and Power

## Energy

Energy is conserved: it cannot be created or destroyed, but it can be transferred or transformed from one form to another. Energy is measured in joules ($\mathrm{J}$).

Energy forms can be categorized across three physical scales:

1. **Macroscopic:** wind, hydropower, tidal energy, and bulk heat transfer.
2. **Molecular:** thermal vibrations and chemical reaction enthalpies.
3. **Subatomic:** electromagnetic radiation, particle emissions, and nuclear binding energies.

Four fundamental energy forms commonly addressed in energy engineering are kinetic, gravitational, electrical, and nuclear energy.

## Work

Work is the energy transferred when a force causes displacement. For a constant force acting in the direction of displacement:

$$
W = F\,x
$$

For a variable force along a displacement path:

$$
W = \int_{a}^{b} F(x)\,dx
$$

More generally, work is the line integral of force along a displacement vector:

$$
W = \int \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r}
$$

Only the component of force parallel to the displacement does mechanical work.

## Force

A net force produces acceleration according to Newton's second law:

$$
F_{\mathrm{net}} = ma
$$

A force transfers energy through work only when it produces displacement. An applied force without displacement performs zero mechanical work.

## Power

Power is the rate of energy transfer or work performed over time:

$$
P = \frac{E}{t}
$$

The instantaneous power is:

$$
P = \frac{dE}{dt} = \frac{dW}{dt}
$$

The SI unit of power is the watt ($\mathrm{W} = \mathrm{J/s}$).
