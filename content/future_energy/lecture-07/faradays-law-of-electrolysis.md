# Faraday's Law of Electrolysis

Amount of substance produced at an electrode is proportional to total charge passed.

## Formulas

$$n_{\text{conv}} = \frac{Q}{nF} = \frac{It}{nF} \qquad m_{\text{conv}} = \frac{ItM}{nF} \qquad \dot{n} = \frac{I}{nF}$$

- $n$ = electrons per molecule (e.g. $n=2$ for $\mathrm{H_2}$), $F = 96485\text{ C/mol}$
- Molar mass $M$ from [[chem/lecture-01/moles-and-mass|Moles and Mass]]

## Water Electrolysis Example

$$2\mathrm{H_2O \to 2H_2 + O_2}$$

$$\dot{n}_{\mathrm{H_2}} = \frac{I}{2F} \qquad \dot{n}_{\mathrm{O_2}} = \frac{I}{4F} \implies \frac{\dot{n}_{\mathrm{H_2}}}{\dot{n}_{\mathrm{O_2}}} = 2$$

## Key Point

Faraday's law depends on **current**, not voltage. Higher voltage just increases current (and thus rate), but conversion per coulomb ($1/nF$) is fixed.
