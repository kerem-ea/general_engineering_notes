# Nuclear Economics and System Role

## Role in the Energy System

- About $10\%$ of global electricity, the largest low-carbon source in advanced economies. About $30$ countries use it, from over $50\%$ share in several European countries (France highest) to a few percent. Europe gets about a quarter.
- **Merits:** dispatchable baseload, complements variable renewables (VRE), reliable and grid stabilizing, scalable, small waste volume.
- **Concerns:** safety, waste, proliferation, high construction cost, public opinion.

## Uranium Resources

- Reserves about $6\text{ million t}$ (up to $\text{USD }130/\text{kg U}$), roughly $90\text{ years}$ at current use.
- Light water reactors use only about $0.5\%$ of the energy in natural uranium. Fast reactors raise this by about two orders of magnitude.
- Uranium from seawater would make nuclear effectively renewable.

| Identified Resources | Years of Supply |
|---|---|
| **Current cycle (LWR once-through)** | $100$ |
| **Fast reactors, Pu recycling** | $3070$ |
| **Fast reactors, all actinides recycled** | $24\,000$ |

## Economics

- Unlike wind and solar, nuclear costs have not fallen. New builds in Europe and the USA became more expensive from lost experience, licensing delays and added safety measures.
- Overnight cost ($2017\text{ USD/kW}$): historic US plants up to about $12\,000$, recent EPR and AP1000 projects about $6000\text{ to }8500$, South Korean APR-1400 about $3000\text{ to }4000$.
- About $\frac{2}{3}$ of LCOE is construction and about $20\%$ is operation and maintenance. Fuel and back end are minor.
- 2040 projection (IEA): new nuclear about $100\text{ to }110\text{ USD/MWh}$, lifetime extension only about $43$.
- LCOE ignores balancing cost, and dispatchable power has higher value (VALCOE). Deep decarbonization costs considerably more without nuclear (MIT, 2018). Compare [[future_energy/lecture-05/biopower-economics-and-sustainability-debates|Biopower Economics and Sustainability Debates]].

## Operation

- High fixed and low variable costs make nuclear best as baseload (capacity factor above $90\%$, see [[future_energy/lecture-02/capacity-factor|Capacity Factor]]).
- Load following is possible (France with high share, Germany balancing VRE). A light water reactor ramps about $5\%$ of power per minute. Avoid below $20\%$ power because of xenon poisoning.

## Non-Electricity Uses

- Ship propulsion, district heating, desalination, and process heat from future high temperature reactors.
- **Research reactors** make no electricity. They produce medical isotopes ($^{99\mathrm{m}}\mathrm{Tc}$ from $^{99}\mathrm{Mo}$) and neutrons for materials science, testing and training.
