# Advanced Reactors and Fusion

## Reactor Generations

| Generation | Period | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | 1950s to 1960s | Shippingport, Dresden, Magnox |
| **II** | 1970s to 1990s | PWR, BWR, CANDU |
| **III** | 1990s to 2000s | Advanced LWRs (CANDU6, System 80+) |
| **III+** | 2010s to 2020s | EPR, AP1000, ESBWR, passive safety |
| **IV** | about 2030 onwards | Safer, less waste, better resource use, cheaper, proliferation resistant |

Generation IV has six concepts: SCWR (supercritical water), SFR (sodium fast), GFR (gas fast), LFR (lead fast), VHTR (very high temperature) and MSR (molten salt).

## Design Directions

- **High temperature** ($400\text{ to }\sim 1000\ ^\circ\text{C}$ against $\sim 280\ ^\circ\text{C}$ for LWR): higher efficiency and process heat. Above $\sim 600\ ^\circ\text{C}$ efficient electrolysis, above $\sim 800\ ^\circ\text{C}$ thermolysis, e.g. synthetic fuels.
- **Fast reactors** (Na, Pb or Pb-Bi cooled): $50\text{ to }200\times$ better fuel use via the plutonium cycle, less uranium needed, shorter waste cooling time. Prototypes: Phenix and Superphenix (France), BN800 (Russia), none commercial. See [[neutron-cross-sections-and-fissile-fertile-fuels|Neutron Cross Sections and Fissile and Fertile Fuels]].
- **Molten salt reactor:** fuel dissolved in liquid salt at atmospheric pressure, on-line refueling and fission product removal, fast or thermal spectrum.
- **Subcritical (accelerator-driven):** proton spallation supplies neutrons so $k_{\text{eff}} < 1$ and criticality risk drops. Suits burning minor actinides. Prototype MYRRHA in Belgium.
- **SMR** (below $300\text{ MWe}$): series-built in a factory to cut cost, fits small grids. Example: Akademik Lomonosov, two $70\text{ MWe}$ PWRs on a barge, commissioned 2020.

## Fusion

$$
\mathrm{D} + \mathrm{T} \longrightarrow {}^{4}\mathrm{He} + \mathrm{n} + 17.6\text{ MeV}
$$

- Needs very high temperature so nuclei overcome electrical repulsion, with the fuel as a plasma at low pressure.
- **Confinement:** magnetic (tokamak, stellarator) or inertial (laser compression).
- ITER (tokamak) is under construction in France. A prototype power reactor is expected in $20\text{ to }30\text{ years}$.

> [!note] Fusion temperature
> The reading gives $\sim 10^{10}\text{ K}$, but the usual figure is $\sim 10^8\text{ K}$, about ten times the Sun's core.
