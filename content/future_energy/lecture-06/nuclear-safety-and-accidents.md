# Nuclear Safety and Accidents

## Initiating Events

The main concern is an accident that spreads radioactive material into the environment. Physically two events can start it:

| Event | Cause | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Criticality accident** | Reactor becomes supercritical ($k_{\text{eff}} > 1$), large power excursion | Chernobyl |
| **Loss-of-coolant accident** | Cooling after shutdown is lost | Three Mile Island, Fukushima |

Cooling is needed after shutdown because $10\text{ to }15\%$ of fission energy is released later by fragment decay (see [[binding-energy-and-nuclear-reactions|Binding Energy and Nuclear Reactions]]).

## Safety Design

- Limited capability to reach a supercritical state, with $k_{\text{eff}} < 1.001$ (see [[fission-chain-reaction-and-criticality|Fission Chain Reaction and Criticality]]).
- Redundant auxiliary cooling systems.
- **Defense-in-depth:** a series of protective barriers that keep radioactive material from the environment if an accident happens anyway.
- **Generation III and III+:** passive safety systems (e.g. emergency cooling) that work without human action or external power.

## Fukushima 2011

1. Earthquake: the plants scrammed on loss of primary power, leaving the reactors in a safe state.
2. Tsunami about one hour later: emergency cooling (generators and pumps) was destroyed along with local infrastructure.
3. Result: partial meltdown of four reactors at Fukushima Daiichi and release of radioactivity to the atmosphere.
4. Afterwards: European plants went through stress tests for loss of power and cooling (earthquakes, flooding), and none was closed.
