# Uranium Enrichment and the Nuclear Fuel Cycle

## Enrichment

Uranium-235 is only $0.7\%$ of natural uranium, so it must be concentrated (see [[neutron-cross-sections-and-fissile-fertile-fuels|Neutron Cross Sections and Fissile and Fertile Fuels]]). Isotopes have identical chemistry, so physical mass-based separation is used. Yellow cake ($\mathrm{U_3O_8}$) is converted to gaseous $\mathrm{UF_6}$ first.

| Method             | Principle                                                                                                                                                                       | Status                                        |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| **Gas diffusion**  | Lighter $^{235}\mathrm{UF_6}$ passes membranes slightly faster (Graham's law, see [[chem/lecture-02/effusion-diffusion-of-gasses\|Chemistry: Effusion and Diffusion of Gases]]) | Energy intensive, no longer used commercially |
| **Gas centrifuge** | Heavier $^{238}\mathrm{UF_6}$ is thrown outward, $^{235}\mathrm{UF_6}$ collected near the centre                                                                                | Current method, cascades of thousands         |
| **Laser**          | Photo-ionize uranium-235 selectively                                                                                                                                            | Not industrial yet                            |

### Example: Diffusion Speed Ratio

$$
M(^{235}\mathrm{UF_6}) = 235 + 6 \cdot 19 = 349, \qquad M(^{238}\mathrm{UF_6}) = 352
$$

$$
\frac{v_{235}}{v_{238}} = \sqrt{\frac{352}{349}} \approx 1.0043
$$

Each stage gives only about $0.4\%$ separation, so thousands of passes are needed.

| Category | Uranium-235 Content |
|---|---|
| **Natural uranium** | $0.7\%$ |
| **Power reactors (LWR)** | $3\text{ to }5\%$ |
| **Fast reactors** | $10\text{ to }20\%$ |
| **LEU / HEU** | below $20\%$ / $20\%$ or more |
| **Weapons grade** | $80\text{ to }90\%$ |

## Fuel Cycle Steps

1. **Mining and milling:** crust average $2.8\text{ ppm}$, top mines (Canada, Namibia, Australia, Kazakhstan) above $1000\text{ ppm}$. Tailings contain decay chain products including radon gas (see [[radioactivity-and-decay-chains|Radioactivity and Decay Chains]]).
2. **Conversion and enrichment.**
3. **Fuel fabrication:** $\mathrm{UO_2}$ pellets in fuel rods. A $1000\text{ MW}$ electrical core holds about $75\text{ t}$ of uranium.
4. **Irradiation and burnup:** uranium-235 fissions while uranium-238 breeds plutonium and heavier transuranics. About $50\%$ of the plutonium is burned in the reactor.
5. **After use:** direct disposal (once-through) or reprocessing.

## Reprocessing

- Plutonium and unused uranium are extracted chemically (Sellafield in the UK, La Hague in France) and reused, e.g. as **MOX** fuel. The rest is vitrified for disposal.
- Gives better fuel use and cooler waste that needs shorter storage.

| Strategy | Time for Radiotoxicity to Reach Natural Uranium |
|---|---|
| **Direct disposal** | $\sim 10^5\text{ to }10^6\text{ yr}$ |
| **Recycling plutonium** | $\sim 10^4\text{ yr}$ |
| **Recycling all actinides** | $\sim 300\text{ to }1000\text{ yr}$ |
## Waste Management

- Classes: exempt, low, intermediate and high level.
- Low and intermediate waste goes to near-surface disposal, high-level waste to deep geological repositories.
- The reading (2020) states no country yet had a final repository. Volume is small but highly radioactive.
