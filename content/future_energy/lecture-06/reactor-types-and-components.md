# Reactor Types and Components

## Plant Principle

A nuclear plant is a thermal power plant where the reactor makes the heat (see [[future_energy/lecture-02/efficiency-and-carnot|Heat Engines and Carnot's Limitation]]). In a PWR there are three loops:
1. **Primary:** water through the core and a steam generator.
2. **Secondary:** steam to the turbine and generator.
3. **Third:** condenser cooling, released in a cooling tower or to seawater.

## Reactor Components

- **Fuel rods:** tubes of $\mathrm{UO_2}$, usually enriched in uranium-235.
- **Moderator:** slows neutrons by elastic scattering. Water, graphite or heavy water. Fast reactors have none.
- **Control rods:** neutron absorbers (B, Cd, Hf) that set the fission rate and shut the reactor down.
- **Coolant:** carries heat out. In water reactors it is also the moderator.

## Thermal versus Fast Reactors

| Feature | Thermal (LWR) | Fast |
|---|---|---|
| **Enrichment** | $3\text{ to }5\%$ | $10\text{ to }20\%$ |
| **Moderator** | Yes | No |
| **Neutron spectrum** | Thermal | Hard (fast) |
| **Benefit** | Mature technology | Better neutron economy, efficient plutonium cycle |

## Light Water and Heavy Water Reactors

![[pwr-bwr-nuclear.png]]

| Feature                                | PWR                         | BWR                          | PHWR (CANDU)                  |
| -------------------------------------- | --------------------------- | ---------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| **Coolant and moderator**              | Light water                 | Light water                  | Heavy water ($\mathrm{D_2O}$) |
| **Steam generation**                   | Secondary loop              | Directly in core             | Secondary loop                |
| **Pressure**                           | $150\text{ bar}$            | $70\text{ bar}$              | Pressurized                   |
| **Temperature in / out**               | $285 / 325\ ^\circ\text{C}$ | $173 / 286\ ^\circ\text{C}$  |                               |
| **Pressure vessel (height, diameter)** | $14\text{ m},\ 4\text{ m}$  | $21.5\text{ m},\ 6\text{ m}$ |                               |
| **Fuel**                               | Enriched $\mathrm{UO_2}$    | Enriched $\mathrm{UO_2}$     | Natural uranium               |
| **Plants worldwide**                   | $310\ (74\%)$               | $43\ (10\%)$                 | $46\ (11\%)$                  |
| **Capacity**                           | $298\text{ GW}\ (78\%)$     | $45\text{ GW}\ (12\%)$       | $24\text{ GW}\ (6\%)$         |

Total: $420$ plants and $380\text{ GW}$ electrical (IAEA, 2026). The running text gives slightly different counts, so use the figure.

- **PWR:** water kept liquid under pressure, radioactive water stays in the primary loop.
- **BWR:** simpler, but radioactive water from the core passes through the turbine system.
- **PHWR:** heavy water absorbs fewer neutrons than light water, so the reactor runs on natural uranium (Canadian design).
