# Number Sets

The standard number sets are:

$$
\mathbb N=\{1,2,3,\ldots\}
$$

> [!note] Convention on zero in $\mathbb N$
> The convention adopted here is $\mathbb N = \{1, 2, 3, \ldots\}$ (positive integers). However, in computer science, mathematical logic, and ISO standards (ISO 80000-2), zero is included: $\mathbb N = \{0, 1, 2, \ldots\}$. To prevent ambiguity, texts often use $\mathbb N_0$ or $\mathbb Z_{\ge 0}$ for $\{0, 1, 2, \ldots\}$ and $\mathbb N^*$ or $\mathbb N^+$ for $\{1, 2, 3, \ldots\}$.


$$
\mathbb Z=\{\ldots,-2,-1,0,1,2,\ldots\}
$$

$$
\mathbb Q=\left\{\frac ab\mid a,b\in\mathbb Z,\ b\neq0\right\}
$$

$$
\mathbb R=\text{the real numbers}
$$

They satisfy:

$$
\mathbb N\subsetneq\mathbb Z\subsetneq\mathbb Q\subsetneq\mathbb R
$$

## Set-builder notation

A set can be defined using a condition:

$$
\{x\in A\mid P(x)\}
$$

This means "all $x$ in $A$ for which $P(x)$ is true."
