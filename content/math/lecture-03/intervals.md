# Intervals

Intervals describe continuous subsets of the real [[number-sets|number set]] $\mathbb R$. They represent connected portions of the real number line, frequently used as domains and codomains for [[functions|functions]].

> [!note] Infinity is not a number, so it can never be included in an interval.

## Closed interval

Both endpoints included:

$$
[a,b]=\{x\in\mathbb R\mid a\le x\le b\}
$$

## Half-open intervals

Left endpoint included, right endpoint excluded:

$$
[a,b[=\{x\in\mathbb R\mid a\le x<b\}
$$

Left endpoint excluded, right endpoint included:

$$
]a,b]=\{x\in\mathbb R\mid a<x\le b\}
$$

## Open interval

Neither endpoint included:

$$
]a,b[=\{x\in\mathbb R\mid a<x<b\}
$$

Interval partitions are used to define domain cases in [[math/lecture-02/absolute-value-equations-and-tautologies|Solving Absolute Value Equations via Tautologies]] and to specify restricted invertible branches for [[math/lecture-06/inverse-trigonometric-functions|inverse trigonometric functions]].
