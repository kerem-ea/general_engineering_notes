# Complex Numbers

The complex numbers use their own specific [[complex-arithmetic|arithmetic]].

The complex numbers can be represented graphically on the [[complex-plane|complex plane]] and converted to [[complex-polar-form|polar form]].

## Definition

The [[math/lecture-03/sets|set]] $\mathbb{C}$ of complex numbers is defined as:

$$
\mathbb{C} = \{a + bi \mid a, b \in \mathbb{R}\}
$$

where the number $i$ satisfies:

$$
i^2 = -1
$$

Every real number is also a complex number (with imaginary part zero), extending the numerical chain from [[math/lecture-03/number-sets|Number Sets]]:

$$
\mathbb{R} \subsetneq \mathbb{C}
$$

## Rectangular Form

A complex number $z$ is written as:

$$
z = a + bi
$$

where $a$ is the **real part** and $b$ is the **imaginary part**, denoted:

$$
\operatorname{Re}(z) = a, \qquad \operatorname{Im}(z) = b
$$

The pair $(\operatorname{Re}(z),\, \operatorname{Im}(z))$ are called the **rectangular coordinates** of $z$. If the real and imaginary parts are known, we can write:

$$
z = \operatorname{Re}(z) + \operatorname{Im}(z)\,i
$$

This is known as the **rectangular form**. For flipping the imaginary component, see [[complex-conjugation|Complex Conjugation]].
