# Intermolecular Forces

Intermolecular forces are attractions between molecules. They arise from electrostatic interactions (see [[future_energy/lecture-01/electrical-energy|Electrical Energy]]) and influence properties such as boiling point, melting point, viscosity, and solubility, which dictate [[states-of-matter-and-density|states of matter]].

## London dispersion forces

London dispersion forces arise from temporary fluctuations in electron distribution. They occur between all atoms and molecules.

## Dipole-dipole interactions

Dipole-dipole interactions occur between molecules with permanent electric dipoles created by [[chemical-bonds#Polarity|polar chemical bonds]].

## Hydrogen bonding

Hydrogen bonding is a particularly strong type of intermolecular interaction involving hydrogen bonded to highly electronegative atoms, commonly nitrogen, oxygen, or fluorine, and an electronegative atom on a neighboring molecule.

In gases, intermolecular attractions are responsible for the $a$ correction factor in the [[chem/lecture-02/non-ideal-gas-behavior#The van der Waals Equation|van der Waals equation]] for non-ideal gas behavior.
