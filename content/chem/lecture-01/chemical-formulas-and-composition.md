# Chemical Formulas and Composition

Chemical formulas represent the elemental composition of molecules and compounds.

## Types of chemical formulas

- **Molecular formula** - indicates the actual number of atoms of each element in a molecule of a compound (e.g., $\mathrm{C_6H_{12}O_6}$ for glucose).
- **Empirical formula** - indicates the simplest whole-number ratio of the elements in a compound (e.g., $\mathrm{CH_2O}$ for glucose).
- **Structural formula** - illustrates the connectivity and arrangement of atoms and bonds in a molecule.

## Percent composition

The percent composition by mass of an element in a compound is given by:

$$
\text{\% by mass} = \frac{n \times M_{\mathrm{element}}}{M_{\mathrm{compound}}} \times 100\%
$$

where:

- $n$ is the number of atoms of the element in one formula unit or molecule
- $M_{\mathrm{element}}$ is the molar mass of the element
- $M_{\mathrm{compound}}$ is the molar mass of the compound

The sum of the mass percentages of all elements in a compound equals $100\%$.

## Determining empirical and molecular formulas

### 1. Empirical formula from elemental composition

1. Convert the mass (or mass percentage assuming a $100\ \mathrm{g}$ sample) of each element to moles:
   $$
   n = \frac{m}{M}
   $$
2. Divide all mole values by the smallest number of moles obtained.
3. If necessary, multiply each ratio by an integer (e.g., 2, 3, 4) to obtain the simplest whole-number subscript ratio.

### 2. Molecular formula from empirical formula

The molecular formula is a whole-number multiple $k$ of the empirical formula:

$$
k = \frac{M_{\mathrm{molecular}}}{M_{\mathrm{empirical}}}
$$

where $M_{\mathrm{molecular}}$ is the molar mass of the compound and $M_{\mathrm{empirical}}$ is the mass of the empirical formula unit:

$$
\text{Molecular formula} = (\text{Empirical formula})_k
$$
