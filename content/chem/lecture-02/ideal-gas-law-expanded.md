# The Ideal Gas Law

## Empirical Gas Laws

| Law | Relationship | Constants | Equation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Boyle's Law** | $P \propto \frac{1}{V}$ | $T, n$ | $P_1 V_1 = P_2 V_2$ |
| **Charles's Law** | $V \propto T$ | $P, n$ | $\frac{V_1}{T_1} = \frac{V_2}{T_2}$ |
| **Amontons's Law** | $P \propto T$ | $V, n$ | $\frac{P_1}{T_1} = \frac{P_2}{T_2}$ |
| **Avogadro's Law** | $V \propto n$ | $P, T$ | $\frac{V_1}{n_1} = \frac{V_2}{n_2}$ |

*(Note: Temperature $T$ must always be in **Kelvin**).*

## Combined Gas Law

For a fixed amount of gas (constant $n$):

$$
\frac{P_1 V_1}{T_1} = \frac{P_2 V_2}{T_2}
$$

## The Ideal Gas Law

$$
PV = nRT
$$

- $P$ = pressure ($\text{atm}$ or $\text{kPa}$)
- $V$ = volume ($\text{L}$)
- $n$ = amount of gas ($\text{mol}$)
- $T$ = temperature ($\text{K} = ^\circ\text{C} + 273.15$)
- $R$ = universal gas constant:
  - $0.08206 \text{ L}\cdot\text{atm}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}\cdot\text{K}^{-1}$
  - $8.314 \text{ J}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}\cdot\text{K}^{-1} = 8.314 \text{ kPa}\cdot\text{L}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}\cdot\text{K}^{-1}$

## Standard Temperature and Pressure (STP)

- Conditions: $T = 273.15 \text{ K } (0^\circ\text{C})$ and $P = 1 \text{ atm } (101.325 \text{ kPa})$.
- **Standard molar volume:** $1\text{ mol}$ of ideal gas occupies $V_m \approx 22.4 \text{ L}$ at STP.
