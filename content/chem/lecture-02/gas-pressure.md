# Gas Pressure

**Gas pressure** arises from the force exerted by gas particles colliding with surface walls, as explained by the [[kinetic-molecular-theory|Kinetic-Molecular Theory]]:

$$
P = \frac{F}{A}
$$

where $F$ is force ($\text{N}$) and $A$ is area ($\text{m}^2$). This microscopic force transfers boundary work in thermodynamic systems (see [[future_energy/lecture-02/thermodynamic-systems-and-state-functions#Boundary Expansion Work|Boundary Expansion Work]]).

## Units and Equivalences

$$
1 \text{ atm} = 760 \text{ mmHg} = 760 \text{ torr} = 101{,}325 \text{ Pa} = 101.325 \text{ kPa} = 1.01325 \text{ bar}
$$

*(SI unit: $1\text{ Pa} = 1\text{ N/m}^2$, see [[future_energy/lecture-01/si-units|SI Units]]).*

## Hydrostatic Pressure and Barometers

A **barometer** measures atmospheric pressure via an inverted column of fluid (typically mercury):

$$
P = h \rho g
$$

- $h$ = column height ($\text{m}$)
- $\rho$ = fluid [[chem/lecture-01/states-of-matter-and-density#Density|density]] ($\text{kg/m}^3$)
- $g$ = gravitational acceleration ($9.81\text{ m/s}^2$, see [[future_energy/lecture-01/gravitational-energy|Gravitational Energy]])

## Manometers

A **manometer** measures the pressure of a trapped gas:

- **Closed-end:** Reference arm is evacuated ($P_{\text{ref}} \approx 0$):
  $$P_{\text{gas}} = \Delta h$$
- **Open-end:** Reference arm is open to atmosphere:
  $$P_{\text{gas}} = P_{\text{atm}} \pm \Delta h$$

Gas pressure relates directly to volume and temperature in the [[ideal-gas-law-expanded|Ideal Gas Law]].
