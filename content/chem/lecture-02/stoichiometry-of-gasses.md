# Stoichiometry of Gases

## Gas Density and Molar Mass

Combining the ideal gas law ($n = \frac{m}{\mathcal{M}}$) with density ($d = \frac{m}{V}$):

$$
d = \frac{\mathcal{M} P}{RT} \iff \mathcal{M} = \frac{dRT}{P} = \frac{mRT}{PV}
$$

- $d$ = gas density ($\text{g/L}$)
- $\mathcal{M}$ = molar mass ($\text{g/mol}$)
- $m$ = mass ($\text{g}$)

## Dalton's Law of Partial Pressures

The total pressure of a non-reactive gas mixture equals the sum of each component's partial pressure:

$$
P_{\text{total}} = \sum P_i = P_A + P_B + P_C + \dots
$$

- **Mole Fraction ($X_A$):**
  $$X_A = \frac{n_A}{n_{\text{total}}}$$
- **Partial Pressure ($P_A$):**
  $$P_A = X_A P_{\text{total}}$$

## Gases Collected Over Water

Gases bubbled through water collect water vapor:

$$
P_{\text{total}} = P_{\text{gas}} + P_{H_2O} \implies P_{\text{gas}} = P_{\text{total}} - P_{H_2O}
$$

*(Subtract temperature-dependent vapor pressure of water, $P_{H_2O}$, to obtain dry gas pressure).*

## Gas Stoichiometry

- **At constant $T$ and $P$ (Avogadro's principle):** Volume ratios equal stoichiometric mole ratios directly:
  $$\frac{V_A}{V_B} = \frac{n_A}{n_B}$$
  *(e.g., $C_3H_8(g) + 5O_2(g) \to 3CO_2(g) + 4H_2O$: $1\text{ L } C_3H_8$ requires $5\text{ L } O_2$)*.
- **Combined Mass-Volume Conversions:**
  $$\text{Mass } A \xrightarrow{\div M_A} \text{Moles } A \xrightarrow{\text{mole ratio}} \text{Moles } B \xrightarrow{V = \frac{nRT}{P}} \text{Volume } B$$
