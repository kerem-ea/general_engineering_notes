# Effusion and Diffusion of Gases

## Diffusion and Effusion

- **Diffusion:** Net dispersal of gas molecules from regions of higher concentration to lower concentration driven by random thermal motion.
- **Effusion:** Escape of gas molecules through a pinhole orifice into an evacuated chamber without molecular collisions.

Both processes are governed by the velocity distribution of gas molecules established in the [[kinetic-molecular-theory|Kinetic-Molecular Theory]].

## Graham's Law of Effusion

At constant temperature and [[gas-pressure|pressure]], the effusion rate of a gas is inversely proportional to the square root of its [[chem/lecture-01/moles-and-mass#Molar mass|molar mass]] ($\mathcal{M}$):

$$
\text{Rate} \propto \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathcal{M}}}
$$

For two gases $A$ and $B$:

$$
\frac{\text{Rate}_A}{\text{Rate}_B} = \frac{\text{Time}_B}{\text{Time}_A} = \sqrt{\frac{\mathcal{M}_B}{\mathcal{M}_A}}
$$

- **Principle:** At identical temperatures, lighter gas molecules have higher root-mean-square speeds ($u_{\text{rms}}$), effusing and diffusing faster.

## Example

Ratio of effusion rates of $H_2$ ($\mathcal{M} \approx 2\text{ g/mol}$) to $O_2$ ($\mathcal{M} \approx 32\text{ g/mol}$):

$$
\frac{\text{Rate}_{H_2}}{\text{Rate}_{O_2}} = \sqrt{\frac{32}{2}} = 4 \implies H_2 \text{ effuses } 4\times \text{ faster than } O_2
$$
