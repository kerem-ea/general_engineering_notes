# Acid-Base Reactions

An **acid-base reaction** involves the transfer of a hydrogen ion ($H^+$, a proton) from a donor to an acceptor.

## Acids ($H^+$ Donors)

### Strong Acids

React essentially 100% in water ($\rightarrow$):

$$
HCl(aq) + H_2O(l) \rightarrow Cl^-(aq) + H_3O^+(aq)
$$

![[acid-base.png]]

| Formula | Name |
|---|---|
| $HCl$ | Hydrochloric acid |
| $HBr$ | Hydrobromic acid |
| $HI$ | Hydroiodic acid |
| $HNO_3$ | Nitric acid |
| $HClO_4$ | Perchloric acid |
| $H_2SO_4$ | Sulfuric acid |

### Weak Acids

React only partially at equilibrium ($\rightleftharpoons$):

$$
CH_3CO_2H(aq) + H_2O(l) \rightleftharpoons CH_3CO_2^-(aq) + H_3O^+(aq)
$$

## Bases ($OH^-$ Donors / Proton Acceptors)

### Strong Bases

Dissociate completely to yield $OH^-$ in water:

$$
NaOH(s) \rightarrow Na^+(aq) + OH^-(aq)
$$

### Weak Bases

Partially react with water to produce $OH^-$:

$$
NH_3(aq) + H_2O(l) \rightleftharpoons NH_4^+(aq) + OH^-(aq)
$$

## Neutralization Reactions

An acid and a base react to produce a **salt** (ionic compound) and **water**:

$$
\text{Acid} + \text{Base} \rightarrow \text{Salt} + \text{Water}
$$

$$
Mg(OH)_2(s) + 2HCl(aq) \rightarrow MgCl_2(aq) + 2H_2O(l)
$$

Net ionic:
$$H^+(aq) + OH^-(aq) \rightarrow H_2O(l)$$
