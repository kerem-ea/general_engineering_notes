# Quantitative Chemical Analysis

## Titration

A quantitative volumetric technique where a solution of known concentration (**titrant**) is added to determine the concentration of an unknown (**analyte**):

- **Equivalence Point:** Point where stoichiometrically equivalent amounts of titrant and analyte have reacted.
- **End Point:** The experimentally detected completion point (e.g., indicator color change).
- **Formula:**
  $$n_{\text{analyte}} = c_{\text{titrant}} V_{\text{titrant}} \times \left(\frac{\text{mol analyte}}{\text{mol titrant}}\right)$$
  $$c_{\text{analyte}} = \frac{n_{\text{analyte}}}{V_{\text{analyte}}}$$
  *(Tip: $\text{Molarity} = \frac{\text{mmol}}{\text{mL}}$).*

## Gravimetric Analysis

Separates and isolates the analyte as a solid precipitate of known composition, which is dried and weighed:

$$
\text{Mass \% analyte} = \frac{m_{\text{analyte}}}{m_{\text{sample}}} \times 100\%
$$

## Combustion Analysis

Determines empirical formulas of organic compounds by complete combustion in excess $O_2$:

- **Carbon:** Converted to $CO_2$:
  $$n_{\text{C}} = n_{\text{CO}_2} = \frac{m_{\text{CO}_2}}{M_{\text{CO}_2}}$$
- **Hydrogen:** Converted to $H_2O$:
  $$n_{\text{H}} = 2 \times n_{\text{H}_2\text{O}} = 2 \times \frac{m_{\text{H}_2\text{O}}}{M_{\text{H}_2\text{O}}}$$
- **Other Elements (e.g., O):** Calculated by mass difference:
  $$m_{\text{O}} = m_{\text{sample}} - (m_{\text{C}} + m_{\text{H}})$$
