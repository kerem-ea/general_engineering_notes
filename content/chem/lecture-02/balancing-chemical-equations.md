# Balancing Chemical Equations

Chemical equations must obey the **law of conservation of matter**. Atoms are neither created nor destroyed, so each element must have the same number of atoms on both sides.

## Balancing Procedure

1. Write correct chemical formulas for reactants and products.
2. Balance elements one at a time by adjusting **coefficients** (numbers in front).
3. Ensure coefficients are the smallest whole-number ratio.

> **Rule:** **Change coefficients, never subscripts.** Changing subscripts alters chemical identity (e.g., $H_2O \to H_2O_2$), whereas coefficients only change the quantity ($2H_2O$).

## Example: Combustion of Methane

- **Unbalanced:**
  $$CH_4 + O_2 \rightarrow CO_2 + H_2O$$
  *(Reactants: $1\text{ C}, 4\text{ H}, 2\text{ O}$ vs Products: $1\text{ C}, 2\text{ H}, 3\text{ O}$)*

- **Balanced:**
  $$CH_4 + 2O_2 \rightarrow CO_2 + 2H_2O$$
  *(Both sides: $1\text{ C}, 4\text{ H}, 4\text{ O}$)*
