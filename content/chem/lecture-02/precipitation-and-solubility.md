# Precipitation Reactions

A **precipitation reaction** occurs when aqueous ionic reactants combine to form an **insoluble solid (precipitate)**:

$$
AB(aq) + CD(aq) \rightarrow AD(aq) + CB(s)
$$

## Solubility Rules

| Ion | General Solubility | Insoluble Exceptions |
|---|---|---|
| Group 1 cations, $NH_4^+$ | Soluble | None |
| $NO_3^-$, $C_2H_3O_2^-$, $HCO_3^-$, $ClO_3^-$ | Soluble | None |
| $Cl^-$, $Br^-$, $I^-$ | Soluble | $Ag^+$, $Hg_2^{2+}$, $Pb^{2+}$ |
| $SO_4^{2-}$ | Soluble | $Ag^+$, $Ba^{2+}$, $Ca^{2+}$, $Hg_2^{2+}$, $Pb^{2+}$, $Sr^{2+}$ |
| $CO_3^{2-}$, $CrO_4^{2-}$, $PO_4^{3-}$, $S^{2-}$ | Insoluble | Group 1 cations, $NH_4^+$ |
| $OH^-$ | Insoluble | Group 1 cations, $Ba^{2+}$ |

## Predicting Precipitation

1. Exchange ions (double displacement: $A^+D^- + C^+B^-$).
2. Check solubility table: insoluble product forms precipitate $(s)$.
3. Write net ionic equation:

$$
K_2SO_4(aq) + Ba(NO_3)_2(aq) \rightarrow 2KNO_3(aq) + BaSO_4(s)
$$

$$
Ba^{2+}(aq) + SO_4^{2-}(aq) \rightarrow BaSO_4(s)
$$
