# Complete Ionic Equations

A **complete ionic equation** explicitly shows all dissolved ions in an aqueous reaction.

- **Soluble strong electrolytes** (soluble salts, strong acids, strong bases) dissociate into constituent aqueous ions (e.g., $CaCl_2(aq) \to Ca^{2+}(aq) + 2Cl^-(aq)$).
- **Insoluble solids, pure liquids, weak electrolytes, and gases** do *not* dissociate and remain in intact molecular form (e.g., $AgCl(s)$, $H_2O(l)$, $CO_2(g)$).

## Example

**Molecular Equation:**
$$CaCl_2(aq) + 2AgNO_3(aq) \rightarrow Ca(NO_3)_2(aq) + 2AgCl(s)$$

**Complete Ionic Equation:**
$$Ca^{2+}(aq) + 2Cl^-(aq) + 2Ag^+(aq) + 2NO_3^-(aq) \rightarrow Ca^{2+}(aq) + 2NO_3^-(aq) + 2AgCl(s)$$

> Ions appearing unchanged on both sides ($Ca^{2+}$, $NO_3^-$) are **spectator ions**; eliminating them produces the **net ionic equation**.
