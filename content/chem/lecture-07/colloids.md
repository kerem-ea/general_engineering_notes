# Colloids

A **colloid** is a heterogeneous mixture where dispersed particles are larger than individual molecules ($1 - 1000\text{ nm}$), yet small enough that they do not settle out under gravity.

## Solutions vs Colloids vs Suspensions

| Property | Solution | Colloid | Suspension |
|---|---|---|---|
| Particle Size | $< 1\text{ nm}$ | $1 - 1000\text{ nm}$ | $> 1000\text{ nm}$ |
| Homogeneity | Homogeneous | Borderline / heterogeneous | Heterogeneous |
| Settling | Never settles | Stable (does not settle) | Settles over time |
| Filtration | Passes through paper/membranes | Passes through paper; blocked by dialysis | Filtered by paper |
| Light Effect | Transparent; no scattering | **Tyndall effect** (scatters light) | Opaque; blocks/scatters |
| Examples | Saltwater, glucose | Milk, gelatin, fog, smoke | Muddy water, paint pigment |

## Classification of Colloidal Systems

| Dispersed Phase | Medium | Class | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| Liquid | Gas | Aerosol | Fog, clouds, sprays |
| Solid | Gas | Aerosol | Smoke, airborne dust |
| Gas | Liquid | Foam | Whipped cream, shaving foam |
| Liquid | Liquid | Emulsion | Milk, mayonnaise |
| Solid | Liquid | Sol | Paint, ink, blood |
| Gas | Solid | Solid Foam | Marshmallow, pumice stone |
| Liquid | Solid | Gel | Gelatin, jelly, silica gel |
| Solid | Solid | Solid Sol | Ruby glass, alloys |

## Key Properties

- **Tyndall Effect:** Scattering of visible light by colloidal particles ($1 - 1000\text{ nm}$), making the beam path visible (distinguishes true solutions from colloids).
- **Brownian Motion:** Continuous, erratic zigzag movement of colloidal particles caused by random collisions with solvent molecules, preventing gravitational settling.

## Stabilization and Emulsification

- **Surface Charges:** Colloidal particles adsorb ions or ionize surface groups, creating like charges that repel each other and prevent coalescence.
- **Surfactants & Emulsifiers:** Amphiphilic molecules with a nonpolar (hydrophobic) tail and polar/ionic (hydrophilic) head:
  - Form **micelles** in water: tails point inward trapping grease/oil; heads face outward interacting with water.
  - Examples: soap (sodium stearate), lecithin in egg yolk (stabilizes mayonnaise).

## Coagulation / Flocculation

Aggregation and precipitation of colloidal particles:
- **Heating:** Increases kinetic collision energy, overcoming electrostatic repulsion.
- **Electrolyte Addition:** Multi-valent ions neutralize surface charges (e.g. $\mathrm{Al^{3+}}$ from alum coagulates suspended silt in water treatment).
