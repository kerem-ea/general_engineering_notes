# Intermolecular Forces Expanded

Builds on [[chem/lecture-01/intermolecular-forces|Intermolecular Forces]]. While intramolecular bonds hold atoms together within a molecule (see [[chem/lecture-01/chemical-bonds|Chemical Bonds]]), intermolecular forces (IMFs) are attractive electrostatic interactions between molecules or ions and molecules. They dictate bulk physical properties: boiling/melting points, vapor pressure, viscosity, and surface tension.

All IMFs are electrostatic in nature, governed by Coulomb's law:

$$
E \propto \frac{q_1 q_2}{r}
$$

(see [[future_energy/lecture-01/electrical-energy|Electrical Energy]]).

## Summary of Forces and Structural Trends

### 1. London Dispersion Forces

Present in all atoms and molecules (polar and nonpolar). Arise from temporary fluctuations in electron density producing instantaneous and induced dipoles.

- **Polarizability:** Ease of electron cloud distortion.
  - Increases with molar mass and number of electrons $\implies$ stronger dispersion $\implies$ higher boiling point.
  - Halogen trend: $\mathrm{F_2}\;(-188^\circ\text{C}) < \mathrm{Cl_2}\;(-34^\circ\text{C}) < \mathrm{Br_2}\;(59^\circ\text{C}) < \mathrm{I_2}\;(184^\circ\text{C})$.
- **Molecular Shape:** Unbranched isomers have greater surface contact area than compact spherical isomers:
  - $n\text{-pentane}$ (linear): bp $36.1^\circ\text{C}$
  - Neopentane (spherical): bp $9.5^\circ\text{C}$

### 2. Dipole-Dipole Attractions

Occur exclusively between molecules with permanent dipole moments (see [[chem/lecture-05/molecular-polarity|Molecular Polarity and Dipole Moments]]).

- Oppositely polarized ends attract ($\delta^+ \cdots \delta^-$).
- For species of comparable molar mass, higher dipole moment ($\mu$) yields higher boiling point and enthalpy of vaporization:
  - Propane ($\mu = 0.08\text{ D}$, bp $-42^\circ\text{C}$)
  - Dimethyl ether ($\mu = 1.30\text{ D}$, bp $-24^\circ\text{C}$)
  - Acetonitrile ($\mu = 3.92\text{ D}$, bp $82^\circ\text{C}$)

### 3. Hydrogen Bonding

A particularly strong, directional subset of dipole-dipole interaction.

- **Requirements:**
  1. $\mathrm{H}$ covalently bonded to small, highly electronegative atoms: $\mathrm{N, O,}$ or $\mathrm{F}$.
  2. Adjacent molecule with an available lone pair on an $\mathrm{N, O,}$ or $\mathrm{F}$ atom.
- **Key Consequences:**
  - Extremely high boiling points for $\mathrm{H_2O}, \mathrm{HF}, \mathrm{NH_3}$ compared to heavier group hydrides ($\mathrm{H_2S}, \mathrm{HCl}, \mathrm{PH_3}$).
  - Open hexagonal crystal structure in ice: liquid water is denser than solid ice.
  - Stabilizes secondary protein structures ($\alpha$-helices, $\beta$-sheets) and DNA base pairing ($\mathrm{A-T}, \mathrm{G-C}$).

### 4. Ion-Dipole Forces

Electrostatic attraction between a full ionic charge and the partial charge ($\delta^\pm$) of a polar molecule.

- Responsible for dissolving ionic solids in polar solvents (see [[dissolution-and-solubility|The Dissolution Process and Solubility]]).
- Cations coordinate to $\delta^- \mathrm{O}$ and anions coordinate to $\delta^+ \mathrm{H}$ in water, forming a hydration shell.
- Force increases with higher ionic charge ($z$) and smaller ionic radius ($r$) (higher charge density $z/r$).

## Relative Strengths

| Interaction | Participating Species | Typical Energy ($\text{kJ/mol}$) | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| London Dispersion | All molecules | $0.1 - 30$ | $\mathrm{CH_4 \cdots CH_4}, \mathrm{Ar \cdots Ar}$ |
| Dipole-Dipole | Polar molecules | $2 - 25$ | $\mathrm{HCl \cdots HCl}$ |
| Hydrogen Bond | $\mathrm{H}$ bound to $\mathrm{N, O, F}$ | $10 - 40$ | $\mathrm{H_2O \cdots H_2O}$ |
| Ion-Dipole | Ion + polar molecule | $40 - 100+$ | $\mathrm{Na^+ \cdots H_2O}$ |
| Covalent Bond | Shared electrons | $150 - 1000$ | $\mathrm{C-H}, \mathrm{C-C}$ |
| Ionic Bond | Cation + anion | $400 - 4000$ | $\mathrm{NaCl}$ lattice |
