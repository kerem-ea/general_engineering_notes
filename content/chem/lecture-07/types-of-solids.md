# Types of Crystalline and Amorphous Solids

Builds on [[chem/lecture-01/chemical-bonds|Chemical Bonds]]. Solids are classified by structural order and the forces holding constituent particles together.

## Crystalline vs Amorphous Solids

| Feature | Crystalline Solids | Amorphous Solids |
|---|---|---|
| Structure | Long-range repeating 3D lattice | Disordered, random molecular arrangement |
| Melting Behavior | Sharp, distinct melting point | Softens gradually across a temperature range |
| Cleavage | Clean fracture along lattice planes | Irregular, curved fracture surfaces |
| Examples | Quartz ($\mathrm{SiO_2}$), $\mathrm{NaCl}$, diamond, ice | Window glass, rubber, plastics, obsidian |

## Four Classes of Crystalline Solids

### 1. Covalent Network Solids

Atoms connected throughout the crystal by continuous covalent bonds.

- **Properties:** Extremely hard, very high melting point ($> 1500^\circ\text{C}$), electrical insulators (except graphite).
- **Examples:**
  - Diamond ($\mathrm{C}$): tetrahedral $\mathrm{sp^3}$ network (see [[chem/lecture-05/hybridization|Hybridization]]); hardest natural material.
  - Graphite ($\mathrm{C}$): planar $\mathrm{sp^2}$ sheets with delocalized $\pi$ electrons; conducts electricity in-plane; weak dispersion forces between layers make it a dry lubricant.
  - Quartz ($\mathrm{SiO_2}$) and silicon carbide ($\mathrm{SiC}$).

### 2. Ionic Solids

Cations and anions arranged in alternating crystal lattices held by electrostatic attraction (lattice energy).

- **Properties:** Hard, brittle (mechanical shear aligns like charges, causing cleavage), high melting points ($500 - 2000^\circ\text{C}$).
- **Conductivity:** Insulators as solids (ions locked in place); conduct electricity when molten or dissolved in water.
- **Examples:** $\mathrm{NaCl}, \mathrm{CaF_2}, \mathrm{MgO}, \mathrm{KNO_3}$.

### 3. Metallic Solids

Metal cations embedded in a delocalized "sea of conduction electrons".

- **Properties:** High electrical and thermal conductivity (mobile electrons), malleable and ductile (atoms slide without breaking bonds), metallic luster.
- **Examples:** $\mathrm{Cu}, \mathrm{Fe}, \mathrm{Al}, \mathrm{Au}$, brass ($\mathrm{Cu-Zn}$ alloy).

### 4. Molecular Solids

Discrete molecules held together by [[intermolecular-forces-expanded|Intermolecular Forces Expanded]] (dispersion, dipole-dipole, hydrogen bonding).

- **Properties:** Soft, low melting and boiling points ($< 300^\circ\text{C}$), nonconductive.
- **Examples:** Ice ($\mathrm{H_2O}$), dry ice ($\mathrm{CO_2}$), sucrose ($\mathrm{C_{12}H_{22}O_{11}}$), iodine ($\mathrm{I_2}$).

## Summary Comparison

| Solid Type | Structural Units | Binding Forces | Hardness | Melting Point | Conductivity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Covalent Network | Atoms | Covalent bonds | Very hard | Very high | Insulator (except graphite) |
| Ionic | Cations + anions | Electrostatic (lattice) | Hard, brittle | High | Molten/solution only |
| Metallic | Metal cations | Electron sea | Variable | Variable | Solid and liquid |
| Molecular | Neutral molecules | IMFs | Soft | Low | Insulator |
