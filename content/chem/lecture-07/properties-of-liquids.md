# Properties of Liquids

The microscopic strength and character of [[intermolecular-forces-expanded|Intermolecular Forces Expanded]] govern the macroscopic mechanical and surface properties of liquids.

## Cohesive and Adhesive Forces

- **Cohesive Forces:** Intermolecular attractions between identical liquid molecules.
- **Adhesive Forces:** Intermolecular attractions between liquid molecules and a solid contact surface (such as silica in glass).

### Meniscus and Contact Angle ($\theta$)

- **Concave Meniscus (Water in Glass):** Adhesive forces ($\mathrm{H_2O}$ to glass) > Cohesive forces ($\mathrm{H_2O}$ to $\mathrm{H_2O}$). Contact angle $\theta < 90^\circ$; liquid wets wall.
- **Convex Meniscus (Mercury in Glass):** Cohesive forces ($\mathrm{Hg}$ metallic bonds) > Adhesive forces ($\mathrm{Hg}$ to glass). Contact angle $\theta > 90^\circ$; liquid pulls away from wall.

## Surface Tension ($\gamma$)

Energy needed to increase a liquid's surface area by one unit:

$$
\gamma = \frac{\Delta W}{\Delta A} \quad (\text{J/m}^2 \text{ or } \text{N/m})
$$

- Molecules at the surface lack upper neighbors, experiencing a net inward pull.
- Liquids adopt minimum surface area geometry (spherical droplets).
- Stronger IMFs $\implies$ higher surface tension (water: $\gamma \approx 7.28 \times 10^{-2}\text{ N/m}$ at $20^\circ\text{C}$).

## Capillary Action

Spontaneous liquid flow up a narrow tube against gravity, driven by adhesive forces and surface tension.

**Jurin's Law:**

$$
h = \frac{2\gamma \cos\theta}{\rho g r}
$$

where:
- $h$ = height of liquid column
- $\gamma$ = surface tension
- $\theta$ = contact angle
- $\rho$ = liquid density (see [[chem/lecture-01/states-of-matter-and-density|Density]])
- $g$ = acceleration due to gravity ($9.81\text{ m/s}^2$)
- $r$ = tube radius

Narrower tube ($r \downarrow$) $\implies$ higher rise ($h \uparrow$).

## Viscosity ($\eta$)

A liquid's internal resistance to flow.

- **Intermolecular Forces:** Stronger IMFs $\implies$ higher viscosity (glycerol is viscous; pentane flows easily).
- **Molecular Size/Shape:** Long or branched chains entangle readily, increasing viscosity.
- **Temperature Dependence:**
  $$
  T \uparrow \implies \eta \downarrow
  $$
  Higher thermal kinetic energy enables molecules to overcome intermolecular barriers and slide past one another.
