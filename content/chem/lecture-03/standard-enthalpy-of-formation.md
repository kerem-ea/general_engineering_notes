# Standard Enthalpy of Formation

## Definition

The **standard enthalpy of formation ($\Delta H^\circ_f$)** is the enthalpy change when **exactly $1\text{ mole}$** of a substance is formed from its constituent elements in their standard states ($1\text{ bar}$, $298.15\text{ K}$):

- Units: $\text{kJ/mol}$.
- **Standard Baseline for Elements:**
  $$
  \Delta H^\circ_f = 0 \quad \text{for any pure element in its standard reference state}
  $$
  (e.g., $C(\text{graphite})$, $O_2(g)$, $H_2(g)$, $N_2(g)$, $Fe(s)$, $Na(s)$, $Cl_2(g)$, $Br_2(l)$).

## Formation Equation Rules

1. Product must have a stoichiometric coefficient of **$1$**.
2. Reactants must be uncombined elements in standard physical states (fractional coefficients permitted).

$$
C(\text{graphite}) + O_2(g) \to CO_2(g) \quad \Delta H^\circ_f = -393.5\text{ kJ/mol}
$$
$$
\frac{1}{2}N_2(g) + O_2(g) \to NO_2(g) \quad \Delta H^\circ_f = +33.2\text{ kJ/mol}
$$
$$
2C(\text{graphite}) + 3H_2(g) + \frac{1}{2}O_2(g) \to C_2H_5OH(l) \quad \Delta H^\circ_f = -277.7\text{ kJ/mol}
$$

## Scaling Formation Reactions

$$
q = n_{\text{product}} \times \Delta H^\circ_f
$$

- $\Delta H^\circ_f < 0$: Exothermic formation (compound is thermodynamically more stable than elements).
- $\Delta H^\circ_f > 0$: Endothermic formation (compound stores more energy than elements).
