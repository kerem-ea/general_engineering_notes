# VSEPR Theory and Molecular Geometry

**Valence Shell Electron Pair Repulsion (VSEPR) theory** predicts the three-dimensional geometry of a molecule by minimizing repulsions among all regions of electron density (bonds and lone pairs) around the central atom.

## Electron-Pair Geometry vs Molecular Structure

- **Electron-pair geometry:** arrangement of all electron density regions (bonds + lone pairs).
- **Molecular structure:** arrangement of atoms only; lone pairs are not included.

When there are no lone pairs, the two geometries are identical.

## VSEPR Geometries

| Electron regions | Lone pairs | Electron-pair geometry | Molecular structure | Ideal bond angles |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 0 | Linear | Linear | $180^\circ$ |
| 3 | 0 | Trigonal planar | Trigonal planar | $120^\circ$ |
| 3 | 1 | Trigonal planar | Bent | $< 120^\circ$ |
| 4 | 0 | Tetrahedral | Tetrahedral | $109.5^\circ$ |
| 4 | 1 | Tetrahedral | Trigonal pyramidal | $< 109.5^\circ$ |
| 4 | 2 | Tetrahedral | Bent | $< 109.5^\circ$ |
| 5 | 0 | Trigonal bipyramidal | Trigonal bipyramidal | $90^\circ, 120^\circ$ |
| 5 | 1 | Trigonal bipyramidal | Seesaw | $< 90^\circ, < 120^\circ$ |
| 5 | 2 | Trigonal bipyramidal | T-shaped | $< 90^\circ$ |
| 5 | 3 | Trigonal bipyramidal | Linear | $180^\circ$ |
| 6 | 0 | Octahedral | Octahedral | $90^\circ$ |
| 6 | 1 | Octahedral | Square pyramidal | $< 90^\circ$ |
| 6 | 2 | Octahedral | Square planar | $90^\circ$ |

## Repulsion Ordering

Repulsion strength decreases in this order:

$$
\text{lone pair} - \text{lone pair} > \text{lone pair} - \text{bond pair} > \text{bond pair} - \text{bond pair}
$$

Lone pairs occupy more space than bonding pairs, which compress bond angles below ideal values. In a trigonal bipyramidal arrangement, lone pairs always occupy equatorial positions (more space, $120^\circ$ separation) rather than axial positions ($90^\circ$ separation).

## Multiple Bonds in VSEPR

A double or triple bond counts as **one region** of electron density in VSEPR. However, a multiple bond region repels more strongly than a single bond region, slightly widening adjacent angles.

**Example:** $\text{H}_2\text{CO}$ (formaldehyde) has a trigonal planar electron-pair geometry. The $\text{C=O}$ double bond pushes the $\text{H}-\text{C}-\text{H}$ angle from $120^\circ$ to $118^\circ$ and the $\text{H}-\text{C=O}$ angles to $121^\circ$.

## Procedure for VSEPR

1. Draw the [[chem/lecture-05/lewis-structures|Lewis structure]].
2. Count electron regions around the central atom (each single, double, or triple bond counts as one; each lone pair counts as one).
3. Identify the electron-pair geometry from the table.
4. Remove lone pairs to find the molecular structure.
5. Adjust angles for lone pair repulsion.
