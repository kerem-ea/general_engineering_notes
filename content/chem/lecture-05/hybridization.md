# Hybridization

**Hybridization** is the mathematical mixing (linear combination) of atomic orbitals on the same atom to form new **hybrid orbitals** with shapes and energies suited for bonding. Hybrid orbitals exist only in bonded atoms, not in isolated atoms.

Key principles:
- Number of hybrid orbitals produced equals the number of atomic orbitals mixed.
- All orbitals in a hybrid set are equivalent in shape and energy.
- Hybrid orbitals overlap to form $\sigma$ bonds (and hold lone pairs).
- Unhybridized $p$ orbitals overlap side-by-side to form $\pi$ bonds.
- The hybridization is determined by the electron-pair geometry from [[chem/lecture-05/vsepr-and-molecular-geometry|VSEPR theory]].

## Types of Hybridization

### $sp$ Hybridization

Formed by mixing one $s$ and one $p$ orbital. Produces 2 hybrid orbitals oriented at $180^\circ$ (linear geometry). One $p$ orbital per unhybridized axis remains.

**Examples:** $\text{BeCl}_2$, $\text{CO}_2$, $\text{C}_2\text{H}_2$ (acetylene)

$$
\text{2 electron regions} \implies sp \implies \text{linear}
$$

### $sp^2$ Hybridization

Formed by mixing one $s$ and two $p$ orbitals. Produces 3 hybrid orbitals at $120^\circ$ (trigonal planar), leaving one unhybridized $p$ orbital perpendicular to the plane (available for $\pi$ bonding).

**Examples:** $\text{BH}_3$, $\text{C}_2\text{H}_4$ (ethylene), $\text{SO}_2$, all $\text{sp}^2$ carbons in benzene ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_6$)

$$
\text{3 electron regions} \implies sp^2 \implies \text{trigonal planar}
$$

### $sp^3$ Hybridization

Formed by mixing one $s$ and three $p$ orbitals. Produces 4 hybrid orbitals at $109.5^\circ$ (tetrahedral). No unhybridized $p$ orbitals remain.

**Examples:** $\text{CH}_4$, $\text{NH}_3$, $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ (lone pairs occupy $sp^3$ orbitals)

$$
\text{4 electron regions} \implies sp^3 \implies \text{tetrahedral}
$$

### $sp^3d$ Hybridization

Formed by mixing one $s$, three $p$, and one $d$ orbital. Produces 5 hybrid orbitals (trigonal bipyramidal). Requires $d$ orbitals; applies to period 3+ elements.

**Examples:** $\text{PCl}_5$, $\text{SF}_4$, $\text{ClF}_3$

$$
\text{5 electron regions} \implies sp^3d \implies \text{trigonal bipyramidal}
$$

### $sp^3d^2$ Hybridization

Formed by mixing one $s$, three $p$, and two $d$ orbitals. Produces 6 hybrid orbitals (octahedral).

**Examples:** $\text{SF}_6$, $\text{IF}_5$, $\text{XeF}_4$

$$
\text{6 electron regions} \implies sp^3d^2 \implies \text{octahedral}
$$

## Hybridization and Multiple Bonds

In a $\text{C=C}$ double bond (e.g. ethylene, $\text{C}_2\text{H}_4$):
- Each C is $sp^2$ hybridized: three $sp^2$ orbitals form the $\text{C-H}$ $\sigma$ bonds and one $\text{C-C}$ $\sigma$ bond.
- The remaining unhybridized $p$ orbital on each C overlaps side-by-side to form the $\pi$ bond.

In a $\text{C}\equiv\text{C}$ triple bond (e.g. acetylene, $\text{C}_2\text{H}_2$):
- Each C is $sp$ hybridized: one $\text{C-H}$ $\sigma$ bond and one $\text{C-C}$ $\sigma$ bond.
- Two unhybridized $p$ orbitals per C form two perpendicular $\pi$ bonds.

## Hybridization and Resonance

Resonance does not change the hybridization assignment. Only $\sigma$ bonds, lone pairs, and radicals determine hybridization; $\pi$ bonds (and resonance between them) use unhybridized orbitals and do not affect the count of electron regions. For example, in $\text{SO}_2$ both resonance structures give the same $sp^2$ hybridization on S.

For delocalized $\pi$ electrons in resonance systems like benzene, [[chem/lecture-05/molecular-orbital-theory|Molecular Orbital Theory]] provides a better description.
