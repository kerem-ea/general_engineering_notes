# Formal Charge and Resonance

## Formal Charge

The **formal charge** of an atom is the hypothetical charge it would carry if bonding electrons were split equally between atoms:

$$
\text{FC} = (\text{valence electrons}) - (\text{lone pair electrons}) - \frac{1}{2}(\text{bonding electrons})
$$

The sum of all formal charges must equal the overall charge of the molecule or ion.

Formal charge is a bookkeeping tool; it does not represent real partial charges.

### Choosing the Best Lewis Structure

When multiple valid Lewis structures exist, prefer the one where:

1. All formal charges are zero.
2. If nonzero charges are unavoidable, use the smallest magnitudes.
3. Adjacent atoms bear opposite formal charges (minimizes energy).
4. Negative formal charges sit on the more electronegative atoms.

## Resonance

When two or more Lewis structures with the same atom arrangement but different electron arrangements are equally valid, the molecule is described as a **resonance hybrid**.

$$
\overset{-}{O} - \overset{+}{N} = O \quad \longleftrightarrow \quad O = \overset{+}{N} - \overset{-}{O}
$$

Key points:

- The double-headed arrow ($\longleftrightarrow$) connects resonance forms; it does not mean the molecule switches between them.
- The actual molecule is the **average** of all resonance forms. It does not flip between forms.
- All equivalent bonds in a resonance hybrid are identical in length and strength (intermediate between single and double bond).

### Bond Order from Resonance

For resonance structures, an effective (fractional) bond order can be estimated. For the carbonate ion $\text{CO}_3^{2-}$ with three equivalent resonance forms, each C$-$O bond has an effective order of $\frac{1 + 1 + 2}{3} = \frac{4}{3} \approx 1.33$.

### Resonance and Delocalization

Resonance reflects **electron delocalization**: the $\pi$ electrons are not confined to one bond but spread over multiple atoms. This is treated more rigorously in [[chem/lecture-05/molecular-orbital-theory|Molecular Orbital Theory]], where delocalized $\pi$ molecular orbitals naturally emerge.
