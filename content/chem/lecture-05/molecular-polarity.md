# Molecular Polarity and Dipole Moments

## Electronegativity and Bond Polarity

From [[chem/lecture-01/chemical-bonds|Chemical Bonds]], a **polar covalent bond** arises when bonded atoms differ in electronegativity ($\chi$). The more electronegative atom acquires a partial negative charge ($\delta^-$) and the other a partial positive charge ($\delta^+$).

Electronegativity (Pauling scale) increases across a period and decreases down a group. Fluorine ($\chi = 4.0$) is the most electronegative element.

| Electronegativity Difference | Bond Type | Example |
|---|---|---|
| $\Delta\chi \approx 0$ | Pure (nonpolar) covalent | $\mathrm{H_2}, \mathrm{Cl_2}$ |
| $0 < \Delta\chi \leq 1.7$ | Polar covalent | $\mathrm{HCl}, \mathrm{H_2O}$ |
| $\Delta\chi > 1.7$ | Predominantly ionic | $\mathrm{NaCl}, \mathrm{LiF}$ |

These boundaries are empirical guidelines rather than strict thresholds.

## Bond Dipole Moment

The magnitude of a **bond dipole moment** $\mu$ is:

$$
\mu = Q \cdot r
$$

where $Q$ is the magnitude of the partial charge and $r$ is the bond length. Dipole moments are represented as vectors pointing from $\delta^+$ to $\delta^-$.

## Molecular Dipole Moment

A molecule is **polar** if the vector sum of all bond dipole moments is nonzero:

$$
\vec{\mu}_{\text{net}} = \sum_i \vec{\mu}_i \neq \vec{0}
$$

Two conditions are required for a polar molecule:
1. At least one polar covalent bond must be present.
2. The molecular geometry must be such that bond dipoles do **not** cancel.

### Symmetry and Cancellation

High-symmetry molecules in which all bonds are identical and the geometry is regular are nonpolar even if individual bonds are polar:
- $\text{CO}_2$ (linear): the two $\text{C=O}$ dipoles point in opposite directions and cancel ($\mu = 0$).
- $\text{BF}_3$ (trigonal planar), $\text{CH}_4$ (tetrahedral), $\text{PF}_5$ (trigonal bipyramidal), $\text{SF}_6$ (octahedral): all nonpolar by symmetry.

Molecules with lone pairs or asymmetric substitution are generally polar:
- $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ (bent): two $\text{O-H}$ dipoles add to a net dipole pointing toward O.
- $\text{NH}_3$ (trigonal pyramidal): net dipole toward N.
- $\text{CH}_3\text{Cl}$ (tetrahedral, one Cl): net dipole toward Cl.

Molecular polarity influences physical properties such as boiling point and miscibility (see [[chem/lecture-01/intermolecular-forces|Intermolecular Forces]]).
