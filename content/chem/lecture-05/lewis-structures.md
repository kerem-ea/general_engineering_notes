# Lewis Symbols and Structures

A **Lewis symbol** represents an element by its chemical symbol surrounded by dots indicating its valence electrons (one dot per valence electron).

## Lewis Symbols for Atoms and Ions

From [[chem/lecture-04/electron-configurations-and-aufbau-principle|Electron Configurations]], valence electrons are the outermost electrons. For main-group elements, the valence electron count equals the group number.

- Cations: remove dots corresponding to lost electrons.
- Anions: add dots corresponding to gained electrons.

## Lewis Structures

A **Lewis structure** shows the bonding and lone pairs in a covalent molecule or polyatomic ion. Each pair of shared electrons forms a bond; unshared pairs are **lone pairs**.

- **Single bond:** one shared pair ($-$).
- **Double bond:** two shared pairs ($=$).
- **Triple bond:** three shared pairs ($\equiv$).

## The Octet Rule

Most main-group atoms form bonds until they are surrounded by 8 valence electrons (a filled $s$ and $p$ subshell, matching the noble gas configuration).

**Exceptions:**

| Category | Example | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Odd-electron (radicals) | $\text{NO}$ | Odd total $e^-$; one atom cannot reach octet |
| Electron-deficient | $\text{BF}_3$, $\text{BeH}_2$ | Central atom has fewer than 8 electrons |
| Hypervalent | $\text{PCl}_5$, $\text{SF}_6$ | Central atom (period $\geq 3$) expands octet using empty $d$ orbitals |

## Drawing Lewis Structures: Step-by-Step

1. Count total valence electrons. For cations subtract one electron per positive charge; for anions add one per negative charge.
2. Draw a skeleton with the least electronegative atom central. Connect each terminal atom with a single bond. (H and F are never central.)
3. Distribute remaining electrons as lone pairs on terminal atoms to complete their octets.
4. Place any leftover electrons on the central atom.
5. If the central atom lacks an octet, convert lone pairs from outer atoms into multiple bonds until octets are satisfied.

## Bond Order

The **bond order** is the number of bonding pairs shared between two atoms (1 for single, 2 for double, 3 for triple). Higher bond order means a shorter, stronger bond (see [[chem/lecture-05/bond-strengths|Bond Strengths and Bond Energies]]).
