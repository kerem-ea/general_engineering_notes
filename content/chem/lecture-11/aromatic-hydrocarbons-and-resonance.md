# Aromatic Hydrocarbons and Resonance

(OpenStax Chemistry 2e, section 20.1, pages 972–976)

Aromatic hydrocarbons, or arenes, are cyclic unsaturated compounds that display unique chemical stability and unreactivity compared to typical alkenes. The archetype of all aromatic systems is benzene ($\mathrm{C_6H_6}$).

## Structure of Benzene and Resonance Delocalization

Benzene consists of a planar hexagonal ring of six $sp^2$-hybridized carbon atoms, with bond angles of exactly $120^\circ$. Each carbon forms three localized in-plane $\sigma$ bonds:
- Two $\mathrm{C}-\mathrm{C}$ $\sigma$ bonds to adjacent ring carbons.
- One $\mathrm{C}-\mathrm{H}$ $\sigma$ bond to an outer hydrogen atom.

This leaves one unhybridized $2p_z$ atomic orbital on each carbon atom perpendicular to the ring plane (see [[chem/lecture-05/hybridization|Hybridization]]).

```
         H
         |
         C
       /   \
     C       C
     ||      |     <--->   [ Kekule Resonance Structures ]
     C       C
       \   /
         C
         |
         H
```

Instead of alternating short double bonds ($134\text{ pm}$) and long single bonds ($154\text{ pm}$), X-ray crystallography proves that all six $\mathrm{C}-\mathrm{C}$ bonds in benzene are identical in length ($139\text{ pm}$), intermediate between single and double bonds.

The six $2p_z$ orbitals overlap sideways around the entire ring, forming a continuous doughnut-shaped delocalized $\pi$-electron cloud above and below the molecular plane (see [[chem/lecture-05/formal-charge-and-resonance|Formal Charge and Resonance]]).

## Aromatic Stability and Reactivity

Delocalization of $\pi$ electrons confers substantial thermodynamic stability, known as resonance energy (approximately $150\text{ kJ/mol}$).

Because of this stability:
- Benzene does **not** decolorize bromine water or undergo typical alkene addition reactions. Addition would disrupt the delocalized $\pi$ ring system.
- Instead, aromatic compounds preferentially undergo **electrophilic aromatic substitution** reactions, where a ring hydrogen is replaced by an electrophile while preserving the continuous aromatic ring.
