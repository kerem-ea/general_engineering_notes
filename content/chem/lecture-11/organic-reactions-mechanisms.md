# Organic Reaction Mechanisms

(OpenStax Chemistry 2e, sections 20.1–20.3, pages 969–975, 984–989)

Organic chemical reactions fall into distinct mechanistic categories governed by bond saturation, bond polarities, and molecular orbital interactions.

## 1. Combustion Reactions

All hydrocarbons undergo rapid, highly exothermic oxidation in excess oxygen, producing carbon dioxide and water vapor (see [[future_energy/lecture-02/combustion-and-heating-values|Enthalpy of Combustion]]):

$$
\mathrm{C}_n\mathrm{H}_{2n+2} + \left(\frac{3n+1}{2}\right)\mathrm{O_2} \longrightarrow n\,\mathrm{CO_2} + (n+1)\,\mathrm{H_2O} + \Delta H_{\text{comb}}
$$

Under oxygen-deficient conditions, incomplete combustion generates toxic carbon monoxide ($\mathrm{CO}$) or elemental carbon soot.

## 2. Substitution Reactions

A substitution reaction replaces one atom or group in a molecule with another atom or group without altering the carbon skeleton's degree of saturation.

### Alkane Radical Halogenation
Alkanes are chemically unreactive toward polar reagents. In the presence of ultraviolet light or elevated temperatures, alkanes undergo free-radical halogenation:

$$
\mathrm{CH_4} + \mathrm{Cl_2} \xrightarrow{h\nu} \mathrm{CH_3Cl} + \mathrm{HCl}
$$

### Electrophilic Aromatic Substitution (EAS)
Aromatic rings preserve their delocalized $6\pi$ electron system by undergoing substitution rather than addition. In the presence of a Lewis acid catalyst (such as $\mathrm{FeBr_3}$ or $\mathrm{AlCl_3}$):

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_6} + \mathrm{Br_2} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{FeBr_3}} \mathrm{C_6H_5Br} + \mathrm{HBr}
$$

Nitration ($\mathrm{HNO_3 / H_2SO_4}$) and sulfonation ($\mathrm{SO_3 / H_2SO_4}$) follow the identical electrophilic substitution mechanism.

## 3. Addition Reactions

Addition reactions occur across the reactive $\pi$ bonds of unsaturated alkenes and alkynes. The weaker $\pi$ bond ($264\text{ kJ/mol}$) is broken and replaced by two stronger $\sigma$ bonds ($347\text{ kJ/mol}$), changing the carbon hybridization from $sp^2$ to $sp^3$.

| Reaction Type | Reagent | General Equation | Catalytic Condition |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Halogenation** | $\mathrm{X_2}$ ($\mathrm{Br_2}, \mathrm{Cl_2}$) | $\mathrm{R-CH=CH_2} + \mathrm{Br_2} \to \mathrm{R-CHBr-CH_2Br}$ | Ambient, test for unsaturation |
| **Hydrogenation** | $\mathrm{H_2}$ | $\mathrm{R-CH=CH_2} + \mathrm{H_2} \to \mathrm{R-CH_2-CH_3}$ | Metal catalyst ($\mathrm{Pt}, \mathrm{Pd}, \mathrm{Ni}$) |
| **Hydration** | $\mathrm{H_2O}$ | $\mathrm{R-CH=CH_2} + \mathrm{H_2O} \to \mathrm{R-CH(OH)-CH_3}$ | Acid catalyst ($\mathrm{H_3PO_4}$ or $\mathrm{H_2SO_4}$) |
| **Hydrohalogenation** | $\mathrm{HX}$ ($\mathrm{HCl}, \mathrm{HBr}$) | $\mathrm{R-CH=CH_2} + \mathrm{HCl} \to \mathrm{R-CHCl-CH_3}$ | Follows Markovnikov's rule |

### Markovnikov's Rule
In the addition of an unsymmetrical reagent ($\mathrm{HX}$) to an unsymmetrical alkene, the acidic hydrogen atom binds to the carbon that already holds more hydrogen atoms, yielding the more substituted, stable carbocation intermediate.

## 4. Condensation Reactions

Condensation combines two independent organic molecules into a single larger molecule with the concurrent elimination of a small, stable molecule such as water ($\mathrm{H_2O}$):
- **Esterification:** $\text{Carboxylic Acid} + \text{Alcohol} \rightleftharpoons \text{Ester} + \mathrm{H_2O}$.
- **Amidation:** $\text{Carboxylic Acid} + \text{Amine} \longrightarrow \text{Amide} + \mathrm{H_2O}$.
