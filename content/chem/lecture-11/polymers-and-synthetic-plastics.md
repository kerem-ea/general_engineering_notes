# Polymers and Synthetic Plastics

(OpenStax Chemistry 2e, section 20.1, pages 976, 990–992)

Polymers (from Greek *poly* meaning "many" and *meros* meaning "parts") are high-molecular-mass macromolecules constructed by covalently linking hundreds to thousands of repeating chemical subunits called monomers.

## 1. Addition (Chain-Growth) Polymers

Addition polymers form when unsaturated alkene monomers undergo repeated addition reactions across their carbon-carbon double bonds without generating any secondary molecular byproducts. The empirical formula of the resulting polymer matches that of the original monomer.

### General Reaction Mechanism

$$
n\,\mathrm{CH_2=CH-R} \xrightarrow{\text{initiator}} -[-\mathrm{CH_2-CH(R)}-]_n-
$$

### Major Industrial Addition Polymers

| Polymer | Monomer Structure | Monomer Name | Properties and Applications |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Polyethylene (PE)** | $\mathrm{CH_2=CH_2}$ | Ethylene | Moisture-resistant, flexible. Low density (LDPE) for plastic bags; high density (HDPE) for milk jugs and pipes. |
| **Polypropylene (PP)** | $\mathrm{CH_2=CH(CH_3)}$ | Propylene | High thermal resistance, fatigue-proof. Food containers, automotive battery casings, ropes. |
| **Polyvinyl Chloride (PVC)** | $\mathrm{CH_2=CH(Cl)}$ | Vinyl chloride | Rigid, chemically inert, fire-retardant. Plumbing pipes, building siding, electrical wire insulation. |
| **Polystyrene (PS)** | $\mathrm{CH_2=CH(C_6H_5)}$ | Styrene | Brittle, clear, insulating foam. Disposable cutlery, packaging peanuts, thermal building insulation. |
| **Polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE)** | $\mathrm{CF_2=CF_2}$ | Tetrafluoroethylene | Extremely low friction, non-stick, chemical proof. Teflon cookware, chemical valves, gasket seals. |

## 2. Condensation (Step-Growth) Polymers

Condensation polymers form when multifunctional monomers bearing at least two reactive end groups react together with the simultaneous elimination of small byproduct molecules, typically water ($\mathrm{H_2O}$) or hydrogen chloride ($\mathrm{HCl}$).

### Polyamides (Nylons)
Formed by alternating diamine and dicarboxylic acid monomers, linked through amide bonds:

$$
n\,\mathrm{H_2N-(CH_2)_6-NH_2} + n\,\mathrm{HOOC-(CH_2)_4-COOH} \longrightarrow -[-\mathrm{NH-(CH_2)_6-NH-CO-(CH_2)_4-CO}-]_n- + 2n\,\mathrm{H_2O}
$$

Interchain hydrogen bonds between carbonyl oxygens and amide hydrogens provide exceptional mechanical tensile strength in synthetic fibers (Nylon 6,6, Kevlar).

### Polyesters (PET)
Formed by linking diols with dicarboxylic acids through ester bonds:

$$
n\,\mathrm{HO-CH_2CH_2-OH} + n\,\mathrm{HOOC-C_6H_4-COOH} \longrightarrow -[-\mathrm{O-CH_2CH_2-O-CO-C_6H_4-CO}-]_n- + 2n\,\mathrm{H_2O}
$$

Polyethylene terephthalate (PET) is the standard polymer for beverage bottles and Dacron fabric.

## Plastic Classification and Recycling

- **Thermoplastics:** Linear or branched polymers (PE, PP, PET, PVC) that soften when heated and harden upon cooling, allowing repeated thermal reprocessing and mechanical recycling.
- **Thermosets:** Permanently crosslinked 3D networks (epoxies, vulcanized rubber) that decompose rather than melt when heated; cannot be melted and reshaped.
