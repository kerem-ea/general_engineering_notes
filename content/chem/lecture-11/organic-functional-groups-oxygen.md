# Oxygen-Containing Functional Groups

(OpenStax Chemistry 2e, sections 20.2 and 20.3, pages 977–985)

Functional groups are specific groupings of atoms that exhibit characteristic chemical reactivity regardless of the larger carbon skeleton to which they are attached. Oxygen-containing organic families are distinguished by carbon-oxygen single ($\mathrm{C}-\mathrm{O}$) and double ($\mathrm{C}=\mathrm{O}$) bonds.

## Overview of Oxygen Functional Families

| Family | General Formula | Functional Group Structure | Systematic Suffix | Characteristic Properties |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Alcohols** | $\mathrm{R}-\mathrm{OH}$ | Hydroxyl ($-\mathrm{OH}$) | *-ol* | Strong intermolecular hydrogen bonding, elevated boiling points |
| **Ethers** | $\mathrm{R}-\mathrm{O}-\mathrm{R}'$ | Ether link ($-\mathrm{O}-$) | *-ether* (or *alkoxy-*) | Weak dipole interactions, volatile, inert reaction solvents |
| **Aldehydes** | $\mathrm{R}-\mathrm{CHO}$ | Carbonyl ($\mathrm{C=O}$) at terminal position | *-al* | Polar, prone to oxidation to carboxylic acids |
| **Ketones** | $\mathrm{R}-\mathrm{CO}-\mathrm{R}'$ | Carbonyl ($\mathrm{C=O}$) at interior position | *-one* | Polar, resistant to mild oxidation, industrial solvents |
| **Carboxylic Acids** | $\mathrm{R}-\mathrm{COOH}$ | Carboxyl ($-\mathrm{COOH}$) | *-oic acid* | Weak Brønsted acids, strong hydrogen-bonded dimers |
| **Esters** | $\mathrm{R}-\mathrm{COO}-\mathrm{R}'$ | Ester ($-\mathrm{COO}-$) | *-oate* | Pleasant fruity fragrances, fats and biological lipids |

## 1. Alcohols and Ethers

### Alcohol Classification
Alcohols are classified by the number of carbon atoms attached to the carbon bearing the $-\mathrm{OH}$ group:
- **Primary ($1^\circ$):** $\mathrm{R-CH_2-OH}$ (e.g. ethanol $\mathrm{CH_3CH_2OH}$).
- **Secondary ($2^\circ$):** $\mathrm{R_2CH-OH}$ (e.g. 2-propanol / isopropanol).
- **Tertiary ($3^\circ$):** $\mathrm{R_3C-OH}$ (e.g. 2-methyl-2-propanol / *tert*-butanol).

Because oxygen is substantially more electronegative than hydrogen and carbon, alcohols form strong intermolecular hydrogen bonds (see [[chem/lecture-01/intermolecular-forces|Intermolecular Forces]]), giving them much higher boiling points than alkanes or ethers of comparable molar mass.

### Ether Synthesis
Ethers are synthesized by acid-catalyzed intermolecular condensation of alcohols with elimination of water:

$$
2\,\mathrm{CH_3CH_2OH} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{H_2SO_4,\ 140^\circ C}} \mathrm{CH_3CH_2-O-CH_2CH_3} + \mathrm{H_2O}
$$

## 2. Carbonyl Compounds: Aldehydes and Ketones

The carbonyl group ($\mathrm{C=O}$) features an $sp^2$-hybridized carbon atom bonded to oxygen via one $\sigma$ bond and one $\pi$ bond. The strong electronegativity difference polarizes the bond ($\mathrm{C}^{\delta+} = \mathrm{O}^{\delta-}$), making the carbonyl carbon an electrophilic center susceptible to nucleophilic attack.

- **Aldehydes:** Synthesized by controlled oxidation of primary alcohols:
  $$
  \mathrm{R-CH_2OH} \xrightarrow{[\mathrm{O}]} \mathrm{R-CHO} + \mathrm{H_2O}
  $$
- **Ketones:** Synthesized by oxidation of secondary alcohols:
  $$
  \mathrm{R-CH(OH)-R'} \xrightarrow{[\mathrm{O}]} \mathrm{R-CO-R'} + \mathrm{H_2O}
  $$

## 3. Carboxylic Acids and Esters

### Carboxylic Acid Acidity
Carboxylic acids ionize partially in aqueous solution:

$$
\mathrm{R-COOH} + \mathrm{H_2O} \rightleftharpoons \mathrm{R-COO^-} + \mathrm{H_3O^+}
$$

The carboxylate conjugate base ($\mathrm{R-COO^-}$) is stabilized by resonance across the two oxygen atoms, making carboxylic acids significantly more acidic than alcohols (see [[chem/lecture-09/bronsted-lowry-and-lewis-acids-bases|Brønsted-Lowry Acids and Bases]]).

### Esterification Reaction
Carboxylic acids react reversibly with alcohols under acid catalysis to produce esters and water in a condensation reaction:

$$
\mathrm{CH_3COOH} + \mathrm{CH_3CH_2OH} \xrightleftharpoons{\mathrm{H^+}} \mathrm{CH_3COOCH_2CH_3} + \mathrm{H_2O}
$$
