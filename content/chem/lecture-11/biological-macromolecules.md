# Biological Macromolecules

(OpenStax Chemistry 2e, sections 20.2–20.4, pages 980, 991–994)

Biological macromolecules are natural biopolymers categorized into four foundational classes: carbohydrates, proteins, lipids, and nucleic acids. Each class performs specialized structural, catalytic, energetic, or genetic roles in living systems.

## Comparative Overview of Biomolecules

| Biomolecule Class | Monomer Subunit | Defining Linkage Bond | Primary Biological Role |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Carbohydrates** | Monosaccharides (simple sugars) | Glycosidic bond (ether link) | Rapid chemical energy storage, plant cell wall structure |
| **Proteins** | Amino acids ($20$ standard types) | Peptide bond (amide link) | Enzymatic catalysis, cellular transport, structural tissue |
| **Lipids** | Fatty acids and glycerol | Ester bond | Dense energy storage, hydrophobic membrane barriers |
| **Nucleic Acids** | Nucleotides | Phosphodiester bond | Genetic information storage and protein expression |

## 1. Carbohydrates

Carbohydrates have the general empirical formula $\mathrm{C}_m(\mathrm{H_2O})_n$.
- **Monosaccharides:** Hexose sugars including glucose and fructose ($\mathrm{C_6H_{12}O_6}$). In aqueous solution, straight-chain aldehydes and ketones undergo intramolecular cyclization to form cyclic hemiacetals.
- **Disaccharides:** Two monosaccharides joined via a condensation glycosidic bond (e.g. sucrose formed from glucose + fructose).
- **Polysaccharides:** Starch and glycogen serve as branched energy storage polymers in plants and animals; cellulose forms linear structural fibers linked by $\beta-1,4$-glycosidic bonds that form hydrogen-bonded microfibrils.

## 2. Proteins

Proteins are linear polymers constructed from twenty standard $\alpha$-amino acids. Each amino acid possesses a central chiral $\alpha$-carbon bonded to an amino group ($-\mathrm{NH_2}$), a carboxyl group ($-\mathrm{COOH}$), a hydrogen atom, and a variable side chain ($-\mathrm{R}$).

### Levels of Protein Structure

1. **Primary structure:** The linear sequence of amino acids linked by covalent peptide (amide) bonds ($-\mathrm{CO}-\mathrm{NH}-$).
2. **Secondary structure:** Local spatial conformations stabilized by hydrogen bonding along the peptide backbone, forming $\alpha$-helices and $\beta$-pleated sheets.
3. **Tertiary structure:** Global three-dimensional folding of the polypeptide chain driven by side-chain R-group interactions (disulfide covalent bridges, ionic salt bridges, hydrophobic collapse, and hydrogen bonding).
4. **Quaternary structure:** Assembly of two or more independent folded polypeptide subunits into a single functional complex (e.g. tetrameric hemoglobin).

## 3. Lipids

Lipids are predominantly nonpolar, hydrophobic molecules soluble in organic solvents.
- **Triglycerides (Fats and Oils):** Formed by the triple esterification of glycerol with three long-chain fatty acid molecules.
  - Saturated fatty acids contain zero double bonds, packing tightly into solids at room temperature.
  - Unsaturated fatty acids contain *cis* double bonds that induce structural kinks, inhibiting crystallization and remaining liquid oils.
- **Phospholipids:** Composed of a glycerol backbone, two hydrophobic fatty acid tails, and a polar, hydrophilic phosphate head group. In aqueous media, phospholipids spontaneously self-assemble into lipid bilayers that constitute biological cell membranes.

## 4. Nucleic Acids

Deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA) and ribonucleic acid (RNA) encode and translate genetic information.
- **Nucleotide Monomer:** Consists of three components:
  1. A pentose sugar (2-deoxyribose in DNA; ribose in RNA).
  2. An inorganic phosphate group.
  3. A nitrogenous base: purines (Adenine, Guanine) and pyrimidines (Cytosine, Thymine in DNA, Uracil in RNA).
- **Double Helix:** Two antiparallel polynucleotide chains are linked along their exterior by alternating sugar-phosphate phosphodiester bonds. The interior bases pair through specific hydrogen bonding: Adenine pairs with Thymine ($2$ hydrogen bonds), and Guanine pairs with Cytosine ($3$ hydrogen bonds).
