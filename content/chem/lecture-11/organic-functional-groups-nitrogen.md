# Nitrogen-Containing Functional Groups

(OpenStax Chemistry 2e, section 20.4, pages 986–991)

Nitrogen atoms impart distinct acid-base properties and hydrogen-bonding characteristics to organic compounds. The two primary nitrogen-containing organic families are amines and amides.

## Amines

Amines are organic derivatives of ammonia ($\mathrm{NH_3}$) in which one or more hydrogen atoms are replaced by alkyl or aryl hydrocarbon groups.

### Classification

| Type | General Formula | Example | Systematic Name |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary ($1^\circ$)** | $\mathrm{R-NH_2}$ | $\mathrm{CH_3-NH_2}$ | Methanamine (methylamine) |
| **Secondary ($2^\circ$)** | $\mathrm{R-NH-R'}$ | $\mathrm{(CH_3)_2NH}$ | $N$-methylmethanamine (dimethylamine) |
| **Tertiary ($3^\circ$)** | $\mathrm{R-N(R')-R''}$ | $\mathrm{(CH_3)_3N}$ | $N,N$-dimethylmethanamine (trimethylamine) |

### Basicity and Hydrogen Bonding

Nitrogen possesses an unshared valence electron lone pair (see [[chem/lecture-05/lewis-structures|Lewis Structures]]). Consequently:
- Amines act as **weak Brønsted-Lowry and Lewis bases** in aqueous solution, accepting a proton:
  $$
  \mathrm{R-NH_2} + \mathrm{H_2O} \rightleftharpoons \mathrm{R-NH_3^+} + \mathrm{OH^-}
  $$
- Primary and secondary amines engage in intermolecular hydrogen bonding via $\mathrm{N}-\mathrm{H} \cdots \mathrm{N}$ interactions. Because nitrogen is less electronegative than oxygen, these hydrogen bonds are weaker than those in alcohols, resulting in lower boiling points for amines compared to alcohols of similar molecular weight.

## Amides

Amides are carboxylic acid derivatives in which the hydroxyl group ($-\mathrm{OH}$) is replaced by an amino group ($-\mathrm{NH_2}$, $-\mathrm{NHR}$, or $-\mathrm{NR_2}$). The general functional group is:

$$
\mathrm{R}-\mathrm{C}(=\mathrm{O})-\mathrm{NR'R''}
$$

### Amidation Synthesis

Amides are synthesized through a condensation reaction between a carboxylic acid and an amine (or ammonia) with elimination of a water molecule:

$$
\mathrm{R-COOH} + \mathrm{H-NH-R'} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \mathrm{R-CO-NH-R'} + \mathrm{H_2O}
$$

### Physical Properties and Biological Significance

Unlike amines, amides are **non-basic** in water. The nitrogen lone pair is strongly delocalized into the adjacent carbonyl $\pi$ system via resonance:

$$
\mathrm{R}-\overset{\overset{\displaystyle :O:}{\parallel}}{\mathrm{C}}-\overset{\displaystyle \cdot\cdot}{\mathrm{N}}\mathrm{H_2} \longleftrightarrow \mathrm{R}-\overset{\overset{\displaystyle :\ddot{O}:^-}{\vert}}{\mathrm{C}}=\overset{\displaystyle +}{\mathrm{N}}\mathrm{H_2}
$$

This resonance confers partial double-bond character to the $\mathrm{C}-\mathrm{N}$ bond, preventing free rotation around the amide bond and enforcing planarity. In biochemistry, this rigid planar linkage is known as the **peptide bond**, which forms the structural backbone of all proteins.
