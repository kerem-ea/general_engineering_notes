# Ionization Energy

**Ionization energy** ($IE$) is the minimum energy required to remove the most loosely bound electron from a gaseous atom or ion in its ground electronic state.

## Definitions and Equations

- **First Ionization Energy ($IE_1$):**
  $$
  X(g) \longrightarrow X^+(g) + e^- \quad (\Delta H = IE_1 > 0)
  $$
- **Second Ionization Energy ($IE_2$):**
  $$
  X^+(g) \longrightarrow X^{2+}(g) + e^- \quad (\Delta H = IE_2 > 0)
  $$

Overcoming electrostatic nuclear attraction always requires energy input. Ionization is strictly an **endothermic process** ($IE > 0$, see [[chem/lecture-03/energy-basics#Sign Convention for Heat ($q$)|Energy Basics]] and [[chem/lecture-03/first-law-and-internal-energy|First Law and Internal Energy]]).

## Successive Ionization Energies

Removing electrons from an increasingly positive [[chem/lecture-01/atoms#Ions|cation]] requires greater energy:

$$
IE_1 < IE_2 < IE_3 < \dots
$$

### Core Electron Jump

A sharp, massive jump in ionization energy occurs when all valence electrons are removed and an inner core electron is pulled:

- Magnesium ($[\text{Ne}]3s^2$):
  - $IE_1 = 738\text{ kJ/mol}$ (removes first $3s$ valence electron)
  - $IE_2 = 1451\text{ kJ/mol}$ (removes second $3s$ valence electron)
  - $IE_3 = 7733\text{ kJ/mol}$ (removes first $2p$ core electron: sharp increase)

This jump verifies the valence electron count of the element (see [[chem/lecture-01/periodic-table|Periodic Table]]).

## Periodic Trends in $IE_1$

- **Across a period (left to right):** $IE_1$ generally **increases** because higher $Z_{\text{eff}}$ and smaller radii hold valence electrons more tightly.
- **Down a group (top to bottom):** $IE_1$ generally **decreases** because valence electrons occupy shells of higher $n$ farther from the nucleus.

## Electron Affinity

**Electron affinity** ($EA$) is the energy change associated with adding an electron to a gaseous atom:

$$
X(g) + e^- \longrightarrow X^-(g)
$$

For most neutral atoms, adding an electron is exothermic ($EA < 0$). Halogens have the most negative electron affinities because adding an electron achieves a stable noble gas configuration.
