# Photoelectric Effect and Photons

The **photoelectric effect** is the ejection of electrons from a metal surface exposed to light.

## Einstein's Photon Theory

Albert Einstein modeled light as a stream of localized energy packets called **photons**:

$$
E_{\text{photon}} = h\nu = \frac{hc}{\lambda}
$$

When a photon strikes an electron, it transfers its entire energy in a single event:

- **Threshold Frequency ($\nu_0$):** The minimum frequency needed to eject an electron. No electrons are ejected if $\nu < \nu_0$.
- **Work Function ($\Phi$):** The binding energy of the electron to the metal surface:
  $$
  \Phi = h\nu_0
  $$
- **Electron Kinetic Energy:** Excess photon energy becomes [[future_energy/lecture-01/kinetic-energy|kinetic energy]] of the liberated electron:
  $$
  E_k = \frac{1}{2}m_e v^2 = h\nu - \Phi
  $$

This mechanism also governs photon absorption in [[photovoltaic-principles-and-band-gap|Photovoltaic Principles and Band Gap]].

## Frequency vs Intensity

- **Frequency ($\nu$):** Determines the energy of each individual photon and the kinetic energy of ejected electrons.
- **Intensity:** Determines the number of photons hitting the surface per unit time and the number of ejected electrons. Intensity does not affect individual electron kinetic energy.

## Wave-Particle Duality

**Wave-particle duality** states that light exhibits wave properties (propagation, diffraction, interference) and particle properties (quantized absorption, emission, collisions).
