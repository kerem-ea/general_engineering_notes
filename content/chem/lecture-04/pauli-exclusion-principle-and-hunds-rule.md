# Pauli Exclusion Principle and Hund's Rule

## The Pauli Exclusion Principle

Formulated by Wolfgang Pauli:

> No two electrons in the same atom can possess the exact same set of all four quantum numbers ($n, l, m_l, m_s$).

### Consequences

- Two electrons sharing an orbital ($n, l, m_l$) must have opposite spins ($m_s = +\frac{1}{2}$ and $m_s = -\frac{1}{2}$).
- Any single atomic orbital holds a maximum of **two electrons**.
- Subshell capacities:
  - $s$ subshell (1 orbital): up to 2 electrons
  - $p$ subshell (3 orbitals): up to 6 electrons
  - $d$ subshell (5 orbitals): up to 10 electrons
  - $f$ subshell (7 orbitals): up to 14 electrons

## Hund's Rule

Formulated by Friedrich Hund:

> In degenerate orbitals, the lowest-energy configuration has the maximum number of unpaired electrons with parallel spins.

### Mechanism

Electrons repel each other via Coulomb forces (see [[future_energy/lecture-01/electrical-energy|Coulomb's Law]]). Occupying separate degenerate orbitals minimizes electrostatic repulsion, and parallel spins maximize exchange stabilization.

### Examples

- **Carbon ($Z=6$):** $1s^2 2s^2 2p^2$ has two unpaired electrons in separate $2p$ orbitals.
- **Nitrogen ($Z=7$):** $1s^2 2s^2 2p^3$ has three unpaired electrons with parallel spins (half-filled subshell).
- **Oxygen ($Z=8$):** $1s^2 2s^2 2p^4$ contains one paired $2p$ orbital and two singly occupied $2p$ orbitals.
