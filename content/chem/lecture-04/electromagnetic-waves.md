# Electromagnetic Waves

A **wave** is an oscillation that transports energy through space without permanently displacing matter (see [[future_energy/lecture-01/electromagnetic-radiation|Electromagnetic Radiation]] and [[chem/lecture-03/energy-basics|Energy Basics]]).

## Nature of Electromagnetic Waves

Electromagnetic radiation (James Clerk Maxwell) consists of an electric field oscillating perpendicularly to a magnetic field. Both fields oscillate in phase and propagate perpendicularly to the direction of wave travel.

Electromagnetic waves propagate through a vacuum at the speed of light:

$$
c \approx 2.998 \times 10^8\text{ m/s}
$$

## Wave Characteristics

- **Wavelength ($\lambda$):** Distance between consecutive peaks or troughs (units: $\text{m}$ or $\text{nm}$).
- **Frequency ($\nu$):** Number of wave cycles passing a point per second (unit: $\text{s}^{-1}$ or $\text{Hz}$, see [[future_energy/lecture-01/si-units|SI Units]]).
- **Amplitude:** Magnitude of displacement from the wave center. Amplitude determines wave **intensity** (brightness).

Wavelength and frequency are related by wave speed:

$$
c = \lambda \nu
$$

Because $c$ is constant in a vacuum, $\lambda$ and $\nu$ are inversely proportional:

$$
\lambda = \frac{c}{\nu}, \quad \nu = \frac{c}{\lambda}
$$

## Wave Interference

When two waves travel through the same region of space:

- **Constructive Interference:** In-phase waves align peak-to-peak, increasing amplitude (bright fringes).
- **Destructive Interference:** Out-of-phase waves align peak-to-trough, canceling amplitude (dark fringes).

## Standing Waves and Quantization

**Standing waves** (stationary waves) remain constrained between fixed boundaries. For a system with length $L$ fixed at both ends:

$$
L = n\frac{\lambda}{2}, \quad n = 1, 2, 3, \dots
$$

- **Nodes:** Points along a standing wave with zero amplitude.
- **Node count:** A one-dimensional standing wave has $n - 1$ nodes.
- Energy increases with the number of nodes.

Because only discrete wavelengths fit the boundary conditions, standing waves produce **quantization**.
