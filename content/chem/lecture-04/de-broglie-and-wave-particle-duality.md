# De Broglie Wavelength and Matter Waves

Louis de Broglie postulated that matter particles exhibit wave properties.

## The De Broglie Equation

For a particle of mass $m$ with velocity $v$ and linear momentum $p$ (see [[future_energy/lecture-01/kinetic-energy|Kinetic Energy and Momentum]]):

$$
\lambda = \frac{h}{p} = \frac{h}{mv}
$$

where:
- $\lambda$ is the **de Broglie wavelength** ($\text{m}$).
- $h$ is Planck's constant ($6.626 \times 10^{-34}\text{ J}\cdot\text{s}$).
- $p = mv$ is momentum ($\text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}$).

## Scale Comparison

- **Macroscopic objects:** Large mass produces an imperceptibly small wavelength ($\lambda \approx 10^{-34}\text{ m}$), with no detectable wave behavior.
- **Microscopic particles:** An electron has $\lambda \approx 10^{-10}\text{ m}$ ($1\text{ Å}$), comparable to atomic radii, producing measurable wave diffraction.

## Standing Waves in Orbits

Stable circular orbits require the orbit circumference to equal an integer number of de Broglie wavelengths:

$$
2\pi r = n \lambda, \quad n = 1, 2, 3, \dots
$$

Substituting $\lambda = \frac{h}{m_e v}$:

$$
2\pi r = n \frac{h}{m_e v} \implies m_e v r = n\frac{h}{2\pi}
$$

This explains [[bohr-model|Bohr's angular momentum quantization]] through constructive wave interference.
