# Electron Configurations and Aufbau Principle

An **electron configuration** describes the distribution of electrons among orbitals in an atom.

## Notation

$$
n l^e
$$

where $n$ is the principal quantum shell, $l$ is the subshell letter ($s, p, d, f$), and $e$ is the electron count in that subshell (e.g. $2p^4$).

## The Aufbau Principle

The **Aufbau principle** states that electrons fill available subshells in order of increasing energy:

$$
1s < 2s < 2p < 3s < 3p < 4s < 3d < 4p < 5s < 4d < 5p < 6s < 4f < 5d < 6p
$$

Because $4s$ penetrates closer to the nucleus, it fills before $3d$.

## Valence vs Core Electrons

- **Core Electrons:** Inner filled principal shells that do not participate directly in bonding.
- **Valence Electrons:** Outermost electrons that dictate chemical reactivity, ionization, and bonding (see [[chem/lecture-01/chemical-bonds|Chemical Bonds]]).

### Noble Gas Shorthand

Inner core configurations are abbreviated by the preceding noble gas symbol:
- Sodium ($Z=11$): $[\text{Ne}]3s^1$ (1 valence electron)
- Chlorine ($Z=17$): $[\text{Ne}]3s^2 3p^5$ (7 valence electrons)
- Titanium ($Z=22$): $[\text{Ar}]4s^2 3d^2$ (4 valence electrons)

## Periodic Table Blocks

The structure of the [[chem/lecture-01/periodic-table|Periodic Table]] directly mirrors subshell filling:

1. **Main Group Elements ($s$ and $p$ blocks):** Outermost electrons occupy $s$ and $p$ subshells. Valence electron count corresponds to the group number.
2. **Transition Metals ($d$ block):** Electrons occupy $(n-1)d$ subshells. When forming [[chem/lecture-01/atoms#Ions|cations]], outer $ns$ electrons are lost before $(n-1)d$ electrons.
3. **Inner Transition Elements ($f$ block):** Electrons occupy $(n-2)f$ subshells (Lanthanides $4f$, Actinides $5f$).
