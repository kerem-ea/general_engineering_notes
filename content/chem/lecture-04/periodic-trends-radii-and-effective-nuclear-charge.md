# Periodic Trends in Atomic and Ionic Radii

## Effective Nuclear Charge ($Z_{\text{eff}}$)

$$
Z_{\text{eff}} = Z - S
$$

where $Z$ is the nuclear charge ([[chem/lecture-01/atoms#Atomic number|atomic number]]) and $S$ is core electron shielding.

- **Across a period (left to right):** $Z$ increases while inner shielding remains nearly constant. As a result, **$Z_{\text{eff}}$ increases**, drawing valence electrons closer to the nucleus.
- **Down a group (top to bottom):** $n$ increases, adding new principal shells farther from the nucleus.

## Covalent Radius

The **covalent radius** is one-half the distance between nuclei of two identical atoms joined by a [[chem/lecture-01/chemical-bonds#Covalent bonds|covalent bond]].

- **Across a period:** Radius **decreases** left to right due to increasing $Z_{\text{eff}}$.
- **Down a group:** Radius **increases** top to bottom due to increasing shell number $n$.

## Ionic Radii

Forming [[chem/lecture-01/atoms#Ions|ions]] alters electron-electron repulsions and $Z_{\text{eff}}$:

- **Cations ($X^+$):** Removing electrons decreases repulsion and increases $Z_{\text{eff}}$ per electron. Cations are always **smaller** than neutral parent atoms ($r_{\text{cation}} < r_{\text{atom}}$).
- **Anions ($X^-$):** Adding electrons increases repulsion and decreases $Z_{\text{eff}}$ per electron. Anions are always **larger** than neutral parent atoms ($r_{\text{anion}} > r_{\text{atom}}$).

### Isoelectronic Series

An **isoelectronic series** contains chemical species with identical electron configurations (such as $\text{N}^{3-}, \text{O}^{2-}, \text{F}^-, \text{Ne}, \text{Na}^+, \text{Mg}^{2+}, \text{Al}^{3+}$).

In an isoelectronic series, radius decreases as nuclear charge $Z$ increases:

$$
r(\text{N}^{3-}) > r(\text{O}^{2-}) > r(\text{F}^-) > r(\text{Ne}) > r(\text{Na}^+) > r(\text{Mg}^{2+}) > r(\text{Al}^{3+})
$$
