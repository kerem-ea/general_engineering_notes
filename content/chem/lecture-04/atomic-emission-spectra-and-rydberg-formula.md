# Atomic Emission Spectra and Rydberg Formula

## Continuous vs Line Spectra

- **Continuous Spectrum:** An unbroken band of all wavelengths in a spectral region, produced by hot condensed matter (solids, liquids).
- **Line Spectrum (Discrete Spectrum):** Distinct, sharp lines at specific wavelengths separated by dark gaps, produced by excited gaseous atoms or molecules. Each chemical element has a unique line spectrum.

## Hydrogen Emission Spectrum

Passing an electric current through low-pressure $H_2$ gas breaks [[chem/lecture-01/chemical-bonds#Covalent bonds|covalent bonds]], generating excited hydrogen atoms that emit discrete visible lines (Johann Balmer):

$$
\frac{1}{\lambda} = k\left(\frac{1}{2^2} - \frac{1}{n^2}\right), \quad n = 3, 4, 5, 6
$$

## The Rydberg Equation

Johannes Rydberg generalized the emission formula for all spectral series of atomic hydrogen:

$$
\frac{1}{\lambda} = R_\infty \left(\frac{1}{n_1^2} - \frac{1}{n_2^2}\right)
$$

where:
- $n_1$ and $n_2$ are integers with $n_2 > n_1$.
- $n_1 = 1$: Lyman series (ultraviolet).
- $n_1 = 2$: Balmer series (visible).
- $n_1 = 3$: Paschen series (infrared).
- $R_\infty$ is the **Rydberg constant**:

$$
R_\infty = 1.097 \times 10^7\text{ m}^{-1}
$$
