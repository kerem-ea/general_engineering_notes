# Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle

Werner Heisenberg established that conjugate dynamical properties cannot be simultaneously measured with absolute precision.

## Position-Momentum Uncertainty

It is fundamentally impossible to simultaneously determine both the exact position and momentum of a subatomic particle:

$$
\Delta x \cdot \Delta p_x \ge \frac{\hbar}{2} = \frac{h}{4\pi}
$$

where:
- $\Delta x$ is uncertainty in position.
- $\Delta p_x = m \Delta v_x$ is uncertainty in momentum.
- $\hbar = \frac{h}{2\pi} \approx 1.055 \times 10^{-34}\text{ J}\cdot\text{s}$ is the reduced Planck constant.

Decreasing $\Delta x$ forces $\Delta p_x$ to increase. Because of this principle, electrons cannot be described by fixed deterministic trajectories (as in the [[bohr-model|Bohr Model]]), but instead by statistical probability distributions.

## Energy-Time Uncertainty

A related relation connects uncertainty in energy with the lifetime of a quantum state:

$$
\Delta E \cdot \Delta t \ge \frac{\hbar}{2}
$$

States with short lifetimes $\Delta t$ exhibit natural energy line broadening ($\Delta E$).
