# Blackbody Radiation and Planck's Hypothesis

## The Blackbody Concept

A **blackbody** is an idealized emitter and perfect absorber of electromagnetic radiation. When heated, it emits continuous radiation across all spectral regions, called blackbody radiation.

- Higher temperatures yield greater total emission intensity.
- The emission peak shifts toward shorter wavelengths as temperature increases ($\lambda_{\text{max}} \propto \frac{1}{T}$), governed by microscopic [[chem/lecture-02/kinetic-molecular-theory|thermal kinetic energy]].

## Planck's Quantum Hypothesis

Max Planck formulated that vibrating atoms absorb or emit energy only in discrete packets called **quanta**:

$$
E = n h \nu, \quad n = 1, 2, 3, \dots
$$

where:
- $n$ is an integer quantum number ($1, 2, 3, \dots$).
- $\nu$ is the vibrational frequency ($\text{s}^{-1}$).
- $h$ is **Planck's constant**:

$$
h = 6.626 \times 10^{-34}\text{ J}\cdot\text{s}
$$

## Quantization

**Quantization** is the restriction of a physical property to discrete, allowed values rather than a continuous spectrum.
