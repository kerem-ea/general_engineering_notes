# Brønsted-Lowry and Lewis Acids and Bases

Acid-base chemistry describes proton and electron-pair transfer reactions across aqueous and non-aqueous media (see [[chem/lecture-08/chemical-equilibrium-and-constants|Chemical Equilibrium]]).

## Historical Models of Acidity

| Model | Acid Definition | Base Definition | Solvent Scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Arrhenius** | Produces $\mathrm{H^+}$ ($\mathrm{H_3O^+}$) in water | Produces $\mathrm{OH^-}$ in water | Aqueous solutions only |
| **Brønsted-Lowry** | Proton ($\mathrm{H^+}$) donor | Proton ($\mathrm{H^+}$) acceptor | Any protic medium |
| **Lewis** | Electron-pair acceptor | Electron-pair donor | Universal (independent of solvent/protons) |

## Brønsted-Lowry Acid-Base Model

Reactions proceed via reversible proton transfer from an acid to a base:

$$\mathrm{HA(aq) + B(aq) \rightleftharpoons BH^+(aq) + A^-(aq)}$$

### Conjugate Acid-Base Pairs
Two chemical species that differ by exactly one proton ($\mathrm{H^+}$):
- **Acid $\to$ Conjugate Base:** $\mathrm{HA \to A^- + H^+}$ (loss of proton)
- **Base $\to$ Conjugate Acid:** $\mathrm{B + H^+ \to BH^+}$ (gain of proton)

$$\underbrace{\mathrm{HF(aq)}}_{\text{acid}} + \underbrace{\mathrm{H_2O(l)}}_{\text{base}} \rightleftharpoons \underbrace{\mathrm{H_3O^+(aq)}}_{\text{conjugate acid}} + \underbrace{\mathrm{F^-(aq)}}_{\text{conjugate base}}$$

$$\underbrace{\mathrm{NH_3(aq)}}_{\text{base}} + \underbrace{\mathrm{H_2O(l)}}_{\text{acid}} \rightleftharpoons \underbrace{\mathrm{NH_4^+(aq)}}_{\text{conjugate acid}} + \underbrace{\mathrm{OH^-(aq)}}_{\text{conjugate base}}$$

### Amphiprotic (Amphoteric) Species
Substances capable of either donating or accepting a proton depending on the reaction partner:
- **Water ($\mathrm{H_2O}$):** acts as base with $\mathrm{HF}$, acts as acid with $\mathrm{NH_3}$
- **Hydrogen carbonate ($\mathrm{HCO_3^-}$):**
  $$\text{As acid:} \quad \mathrm{HCO_3^- + OH^- \rightleftharpoons CO_3^{2-} + H_2O}$$
  $$\text{As base:} \quad \mathrm{HCO_3^- + H_3O^+ \rightleftharpoons H_2CO_3 + H_2O}$$
- **Dihydrogen phosphate ($\mathrm{H_2PO_4^-}$)** and **hydrogen sulfate ($\mathrm{HSO_4^-}$)**

## Lewis Acid-Base Model

Expands acid-base reactions beyond proton transfers to coordinate covalent bond formation:
- **Lewis base:** species possessing an unshared pair of valence electrons
- **Lewis acid:** species with an accessible vacant orbital or electron-deficient center
- **Lewis adduct:** neutral or ionic complex formed by coordinate covalent (dative) bond sharing

### Canonical Lewis Reactions

#### 1. Electron-Deficient Molecules
Boron trifluoride ($\mathrm{BF_3}$, sextet on boron) accepts a lone pair from ammonia:
$$\mathrm{BF_3 + :NH_3 \to F_3B{-}NH_3}$$

#### 2. Metal Cation Hydration and Complexation
Transition and main-group metal cations act as Lewis acids:
$$\mathrm{Al^{3+} + 6H_2O: \to [Al(H_2O)_6]^{3+}}$$
$$\mathrm{Cu^{2+} + 4:NH_3 \to [Cu(NH_3)_4]^{2+}}$$

#### 3. Acidic Nonmetal Oxides
Carbon dioxide accepts an electron pair from hydroxide:
$$\mathrm{CO_2 + :OH^- \to HCO_3^-}$$
